The never-ending list

When the to-do list never ends

The faucet drips, the car needs work, a surprise bill shows up, and the list just keeps growing. On top of a baby and a job, it can feel like you'll never catch up. You don't have to fix it all at once. Here's how to sort it, so the list stops running you.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Sort it into three piles

  • Now: anything about safety or that gets worse fast. Gas smells, electrical problems, a leak that's spreading, a broken lock, heat in winter, a car that isn't safe to drive. If you smell gas, get everyone out and call 911 or your gas company from outside.
  • Soon: things that will cost more if you wait too long, like a bill with a late fee or a small leak.
  • Someday: everything else. It can wait, and that's okay.

Then pick one thing from "now" or "soon" each week. One done thing feels better than ten half-started ones.

Make repairs smaller

  • Ask for help. A handy friend or family member may be glad to help, especially if you offer something back.
  • Get a pro for the big or risky stuff. Gas, electrical and roof work are worth paying for.
  • Batch small fixes into one short session instead of thinking about them all week.
  • Good enough is fine. A temporary fix can hold until you have more time or money.

When a surprise bill lands

  • Don't ignore it. Open it, and look at the due date.
  • Call early. Many companies, hospitals and utilities offer payment plans or hardship programs if you ask before you fall behind.
  • Check medical bills for errors and ask for an itemized bill.
  • Get local help. 211 is free and confidential, and can connect you with help paying for rent, utilities, food and more. Call or text 211.

For a bigger plan, read money stress with a new baby.

Give yourself a break from the list

The list will always have something on it. That's not a sign you're behind. It's just how houses and families work. Close the list at night, and keep a little time each week that's not about fixing anything. Read carrying the house in your head and no time to yourself.

If it's weighing on you

If worry about money or the house keeps you up at night, or you've felt low most days for more than two weeks, read worn down as a new dad and how to talk to your doctor. Other dads get it too: PSI's free dads' groups meet online, and our community board is open any time.

Common questions

How do I keep up with house repairs with a new baby?

Sort them into now, soon and someday. Handle safety problems first, pick one thing a week, and save big or risky jobs like gas and electrical for a professional.

What should I do about a surprise bill I can't pay?

Don't ignore it. Call early and ask about a payment plan or hardship program, check medical bills for errors, and call or text 211 for local help.

How do I stop feeling behind all the time?

The list will always have something on it. Close it at night, focus on one thing at a time, and keep a little time each week that isn't about fixing anything.
