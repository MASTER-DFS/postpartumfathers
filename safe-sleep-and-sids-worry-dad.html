The baby

Safe sleep and the fear of SIDS

Checking if the baby is breathing. Lying awake listening. Scared to fall asleep with them on you. If that’s you, you’re not alone. Here are the simple rules that keep your baby safe, and what to do with the fear.

If your baby isn’t breathing, is turning blue, or won’t wake up, call 911 now.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Baby on their back, in their own crib, with nothing else in it.
  • If you feel yourself nodding off, put the baby down in the crib. Never on the couch.
  • Set a phone alarm when you sit down for a night feed.

The fear is common

Standing over the crib at 2am, watching for the chest to rise. Waking up in a panic because the baby is quiet. A lot of dads do this, especially in the first few months. It comes from love.

SIDS is rare. And following safe sleep every time lowers the risk a lot. The rules below are the things that actually protect your baby.

Safe sleep, every sleep

From Safe to Sleep and the American Academy of Pediatrics:

  • On their back, for every nap and every night.
  • In their own space: a crib, bassinet or play yard with a firm, flat mattress and a fitted sheet.
  • Nothing else in it. No pillows, blankets, bumpers or stuffed animals.
  • In your room, not your bed. Room sharing for at least the first six months is safest. Bed sharing is not recommended.
  • Not too warm. One layer more than you’re wearing is enough. No hat indoors.
  • Stop swaddling as soon as the baby shows any sign of trying to roll over. That’s usually around 3 or 4 months, and sometimes earlier. No weighted swaddles or sleep sacks.
  • A pacifier at sleep time can help lower the risk. If she’s nursing, wait until breastfeeding is going well.
  • Car seats, swings and bouncers are not for sleep. If the baby falls asleep in one, move them to a flat crib or bassinet when you can.
  • Keep the air smoke-free. No smoking or vaping around the baby, in the house or in the car.

The 2am danger: falling asleep with the baby

This is the one that gets tired dads. You sit down on the couch to feed or rock the baby, and you nod off.

  • Never sleep with the baby on a couch or armchair. It’s one of the most dangerous places for a baby to sleep.
  • If you feel yourself nodding off, put the baby down in the crib right away, even if they fuss.
  • If you might fall asleep feeding, Safe to Sleep says to do it in your bed with the pillows and blankets moved away, not on the couch. When you wake up, move the baby back to their own bed.
  • Set a phone alarm when you sit down for a night feed, in case you drift off.
  • Swap with your partner before you hit the wall. Night shifts mean someone is always rested enough to be safe.

What about baby monitors?

The American Academy of Pediatrics says breathing monitors and wearable devices haven’t been shown to lower the risk of SIDS. A regular monitor to hear the baby is fine. If a device gives you peace of mind, that’s OK, but don’t let it replace safe sleep. Ask your baby’s doctor if you’re unsure.

When the worry won’t switch off

Some checking is normal. But if you can’t sleep even when the baby is safe in the crib, you check over and over, or the fear fills your whole day, anxiety has taken over. It’s common in new dads and it’s very treatable.

  • Remind yourself what you control: back, own crib, nothing in it, in your room. You’ve done it.
  • Take turns with your partner being the one who listens for the baby, so the other can really sleep.
  • Talk to your doctor if it’s gone on more than two weeks. See anxious and on edge and can’t sleep even when the baby does.

In an emergency

If your baby isn’t breathing, is turning blue, or you can’t wake them, call 911 now. The dispatcher will talk you through what to do. A baby CPR class at a local hospital or the Red Cross can help you feel ready.

If the fear has you thinking about suicide, or you can’t cope, call or text 988, free and 24/7.

Common questions

What are the safe sleep rules for a baby?

Baby sleeps on their back for every sleep, in their own crib, bassinet or play yard with a firm, flat mattress and only a fitted sheet. Nothing else in it. Room share, but don't bed share. Never sleep with the baby on a couch or armchair.

Is it safe for the baby to sleep in our bed?

Bed sharing is not recommended. The safest option is the baby in their own crib or bassinet in your room. If you might fall asleep feeding, Safe to Sleep says to do it in your bed with pillows and blankets moved away, not on the couch, and to move the baby back when you wake.

Do baby breathing monitors prevent SIDS?

The American Academy of Pediatrics says breathing monitors and wearable devices haven't been shown to lower the risk of SIDS. Safe sleep is what protects your baby.

Is it normal to keep checking if my baby is breathing?

A lot of new dads do it. If you can't sleep even when the baby is safe, or the worry fills your day for more than two weeks, talk to your doctor. Anxiety in new dads is common and treatable.
