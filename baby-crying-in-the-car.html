Driving with a crying baby

Baby crying in the car? How to stay calm and safe

There are few sounds harder to drive through than a newborn screaming from the back seat. Your heart rate climbs, you want to fix it, and you can't. If your partner is in the car and having a hard time too, it can feel like a lot. Here's the good news: a crying baby who is buckled in is safe, and you can get through the drive.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

The most important thing

Your job is to drive. A baby crying in a properly buckled car seat is safe, even if it doesn't feel that way. Keep your eyes on the road and both hands on the wheel. Don't reach into the back seat, and never take the baby out of the car seat while the car is moving.

If you're too rattled to focus, pull over somewhere safe, put the car in park, and take a minute. Then check on the baby.

Before you go

  • Time drives around the baby. Right after a feed and a diaper change, or at the start of a nap, is often easiest.
  • Check the car seat fit. Straps snug, chest clip at armpit level, and not too hot or cold. Some hospitals, fire stations and certified car seat technicians will check it for you, often for free.
  • Have white noise ready. A white noise app or a "shushing" track through the car speakers can help a lot.
  • Bring a mirror that lets you see the baby at a glance without turning around.
  • Plan a stop on longer drives, so you're not tempted to push through.

While you drive

  • Talk or sing. Your voice can calm the baby, and it calms you too.
  • White noise up, music down.
  • Breathe slowly. In for 4, out for 6. Loosen your grip on the wheel.
  • If someone can sit in the back, they can offer a pacifier or a hand to hold, with the baby staying buckled.
  • Don't drive drowsy. If you're fighting to stay awake, pull over. Exhaustion is a real danger with a newborn at home.

If your partner is struggling too

A screaming baby can be overwhelming for a new mom, especially if she's dealing with postpartum depression or anxiety. You might feel pulled between the road, the baby, and her.

  • Keep your focus on driving. Getting everyone there safely is how you take care of all of them right now.
  • Keep your voice calm. A simple "The baby's safe, we're almost there" can help.
  • Offer a break. If she's in the back, it's okay for her to put on headphones or close her eyes. The baby is buckled and safe.
  • Pull over if either of you needs a minute. There's no prize for getting there on time.
  • Talk about it later, when things are calm, and plan the next drive together.

More on supporting a partner: how to help your wife with postpartum depression. If anyone in the car says they might hurt themselves or the baby, pull over and call 911 or call or text 988.

Common questions

Is it safe to keep driving while my baby cries?

Yes. A baby crying in a properly buckled car seat is safe. Keep your eyes on the road. If you're too stressed to focus, pull over somewhere safe.

How can I calm a crying baby in the car?

Time drives after feeds or at nap time, play white noise, talk or sing, and if someone can sit in the back, have them offer a pacifier with the baby staying buckled.

Should I take my baby out of the car seat if they won't stop crying?

Never while the car is moving. Pull over somewhere safe, park, and then check on the baby.

How can I help my partner if the baby's crying is overwhelming her?

Stay calm and focused on driving, reassure her the baby is safe, offer her a break with headphones, and pull over if either of you needs a minute.
