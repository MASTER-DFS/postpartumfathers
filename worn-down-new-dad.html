Worn down as a new dad? It gets better

If you're short on sleep, short on patience, or feeling a bit on the outside since the baby came, you're in good company. Most new dads go through a stretch like this. It doesn't mean you're a bad father. It means you're giving a lot, and you deserve some support too. Small changes help, and it does get easier.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Why it happens

These feelings rarely come from nowhere. They often build out of things like:

  • No sleep for weeks or months. Everything feels bigger when you're exhausted.
  • Feeling left out. Everyone asks about the mom and the baby. Almost nobody asks how the dad is doing.
  • Carrying more with less time, at home and at work at the same time.
  • Losing your own time. Friends, sleep, exercise and the things that used to help you reset all get squeezed out.
  • Not knowing your role with a newborn, and feeling like you're doing it wrong.
  • Depression of your own. In men, depression often shows up as anger and irritability, not sadness.

What can help

  • Name it to yourself first. "I'm angry and I feel left out" is easier to work with than a feeling with no name.
  • Say it to someone. A friend, another dad, or our message board, where you can say it under a username.
  • Protect some sleep. Taking turns on night shifts so everyone gets one real stretch of sleep can change a lot.
  • Get back one small thing that's yours. A walk, the gym, a call with a friend. Twenty minutes counts.
  • Find your place with the baby. Bath time, a walk with the stroller, or a feed you always do. Small routines help you feel part of it.
  • Get help for you. If the anger won't let up, or you feel numb or hopeless, see your doctor and read the signs of postpartum depression in dads. The PSI directory lists therapists who know the year after a baby. Therapy usually costs money; ask about insurance or a sliding scale.

If it feels like more than a rough patch

If you're afraid you might lose your temper with anyone at home, step away somewhere safe and call or text 988 right now. Wanting help before something happens is the strong thing to do.

You don't have to wait until it's bad to reach out. Talk with other fathers for free: PSI's dads' groups or our community page.

Common questions

Is it normal to feel worn down as a new dad?

Yes, and it usually gets better. It is common, even if few dads say it. Lack of sleep, feeling left out, and carrying more at home and work all feed it. It is a sign you need rest and support, not that you are a bad father.

Can anger be a sign of depression in dads?

Yes. In men, depression often looks like anger, irritability and pulling away rather than sadness. If it has lasted more than two weeks, talk to a doctor.

Why do new dads feel left out?

Most of the attention after a birth goes to the mom and baby, and dads are rarely asked how they are doing. Finding your own routines with the baby and someone to talk to can help.

Where can I talk to other dads about this?

PSI runs free online groups just for fathers, and our message board lets you post any time under a username. If you are in crisis, call or text 988.
