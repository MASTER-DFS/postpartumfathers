Your partner and family

Sex and closeness after the baby

Almost every couple finds things change after a baby, and almost nobody talks about it. If you're wondering when, or whether, things will feel close again, you're not alone. It usually comes back, slowly, when nobody is keeping score.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Take sex off the table for now and say so. It takes the pressure off you both.
  • Keep touch that asks for nothing: a hug, a hand on the back, sitting close.
  • Ask, don't guess: "What would help you feel good right now?"

Her body is still healing

Giving birth, by any route, is a big physical event. Many doctors suggest waiting about four to six weeks before sex, and her doctor or midwife can say what's right for her at the postpartum checkup. Being cleared doesn't mean she's ready. Tiredness, soreness, feeding and stitches can all play a part for months.

A few things are useful to know. Breastfeeding can cause dryness for a while, and a store-bought lubricant can help. Pregnancy can happen before her period comes back, so talk about birth control before you need it. Pain during sex isn't something to push through. She can ask her doctor about it, and pelvic floor physical therapy helps many women.

It changes for dads too

Plenty of dads find their own desire drops. Exhaustion, stress, seeing a hard birth, or simply seeing your partner as "mom" for a while are all common. Some dads worry something is wrong with them. Usually it's just a hard season.

Low mood can play a part as well. Depression, and some medicines used to treat it, can lower desire or cause trouble with erections. If that's happening, tell your doctor. Don't stop a medicine on your own. There are often simple fixes.

How to talk about it

  • Pick a calm moment, not in bed and not after you've been turned down.
  • Lead with closeness, not sex. "I miss us. I'm not in a rush. I just want to feel close to you."
  • Listen more than you talk. She may be touched out, sore, or not feel like herself.
  • No scorekeeping, no sulking. A "not tonight" is an answer, not a rejection of you.
  • Share your side too, gently. Feeling lonely or far apart is worth saying out loud.

Small ways to feel close again

Closeness is bigger than sex. Ten minutes on the couch with phones away. Taking the baby so she can shower in peace. A walk together with the stroller. Saying thank you for something specific. These build the kind of trust that makes the rest easier when you're both ready.

For more on keeping the two of you together, see shutting down or snapping at home and what to say when words fail you.

When to see a doctor or counselor

Talk to a doctor if sex is painful for her, if either of you has no interest at all for months, or if there are problems with erections. If low desire comes with sadness, worry or not feeling like herself, it can be a sign of postpartum depression or anxiety. See helping your partner.

A couples counselor can also help you talk about it without it turning into a fight. That's a normal thing to do, not a sign your relationship is failing. See finding a therapist.

Common questions

How long after the baby can we have sex?

Many doctors suggest waiting about four to six weeks, but her doctor or midwife will say what is right for her at the postpartum checkup. Being cleared does not mean she has to be ready.

Is it normal to have no sex drive after a baby?

Yes, for moms and dads. Tiredness, healing, feeding, stress and low mood all play a part. It usually comes back with time and less pressure.

How do I bring it up without pressuring her?

Pick a calm moment, lead with wanting to feel close rather than with sex, listen more than you talk, and treat "not tonight" as an answer, not a rejection.

When should we talk to a doctor?

If sex is painful, if either of you has no interest for months, if there are erection problems, or if low desire comes with sadness or worry.
