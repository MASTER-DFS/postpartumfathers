Baby number two

Going from one kid to two? You’ve got this

Everyone said the second one is easier. Then you're bouncing a newborn while your older one needs a snack, a bath, and you, all at the same time. Going from one kid to two is a big jump, and it's normal to feel stretched thin. It does settle, and you'll find your rhythm.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Why it can feel harder

  • No more tag-team. With one kid, someone could rest. With two, everyone's on duty.
  • Your older child needs you more, just when the baby needs everyone.
  • Big feelings from the big kid. Tantrums, clinginess, or acting younger are common after a new baby arrives.
  • Less sleep, less space, and less time for yourself than ever.

What helps

  • Become the big kid's person. Many dads take the lead with the older child while the baby is tiny. Bedtime, breakfast, or park trips with Dad can become something special.
  • Ten minutes of one-on-one time a day with your older child goes a long way. Let them pick what you do.
  • Give the big kid a job. Fetching a diaper or choosing the baby's socks helps them feel part of it.
  • Lower the bar. Frozen dinners, more screen time for a few weeks, a messy house. It's survival season.
  • Say yes to help. If someone offers to take the older child for an afternoon, take it.
  • Find a pocket of time for you. A walk, a workout, a quiet coffee. Even short breaks add up.

Looking after yourself

Dads can struggle after any baby, not just the first. If you're feeling worn out, short-tempered or numb for more than a couple of weeks, read worn down as a new dad or the signs of postpartum depression in dads. Talking with other fathers helps too: PSI's free dads' groups meet online, so there's no babysitter needed.

Common questions

Is going from one kid to two harder?

For many families, yes. With two children there's less chance to rest, and the older child often needs more attention just as the baby arrives. It usually settles as routines form.

How can dads help an older child adjust to a new baby?

Take the lead with the older child, give them ten minutes of one-on-one time a day, and give them small jobs that help them feel included.

Can dads get postpartum depression after a second baby?

Yes. Fathers can struggle after any birth, not only the first. If symptoms last more than two weeks, talk to a doctor.
