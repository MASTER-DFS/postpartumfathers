Daycare and child care

Starting daycare

Handing your baby to someone else and walking out the door can feel awful. Dads feel it too, even if nobody expects them to. You might worry they'll cry all day, get sick, or bond with the teacher more than with you. Those feelings are normal. For most families the opening weeks are toughest, and it gets easier.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Keep goodbyes short: a hug, the same few words, and go.
  • Ask the teacher for a photo or update the first few days.
  • Plan something with your baby after pickup, like a walk or bath, so evenings feel like yours.

Choosing care

Look at a few places if you can. Visit, watch how staff talk to the kids, and ask about their training and how many babies each adult looks after. Check that the program is licensed. ChildCare.gov, from the US government, explains licensing, what quality care looks like, and help with paying for child care in your state.

For help with costs, you can also call 211.

Drop-off without the drama

  • Do a few short practice visits before the first full day, if the program allows.
  • Make it a routine. Same order, same words every morning.
  • Don't sneak out. Say goodbye, then leave. It builds trust.
  • Trust the teachers. Many babies cry at drop-off and settle a few minutes later.

If your partner finds drop-off harder, offer to take that job. Many dads find they handle it more easily.

The guilt

Lots of parents feel guilty about daycare. You might hear that a "good parent" would stay home. Working to support your family is part of caring for them. Good care also gives babies other adults who love them, and other kids to watch and learn from. Your baby will still know exactly who their dad is.

The first few months

Expect more colds than you'd like, especially in the first year. Have a backup plan for sick days, and talk with your partner about who stays home when. You may also be more tired than you expected. Mornings get rushed and evenings get short. For the work side, see going back to work after the baby.

When something doesn't feel right

If your baby seems more upset over time, not less, or you see something that worries you, talk to the staff. Trust your gut. ChildCare.gov also has links to report a concern about a child care program.

Common questions

Is it normal for dads to feel guilty about daycare?

Yes. Many parents feel guilt or worry at first. Working to support your family is part of caring for them, and good care gives your baby more people who love them.

How do I make daycare drop-off easier?

Keep goodbyes short and the same each day, don't sneak out, and try a few short practice visits first. Many babies settle a few minutes after a parent leaves.

How do I choose a daycare?

Visit a few places, watch how staff talk to the kids, ask how many babies each adult looks after, and check the program is licensed. ChildCare.gov explains licensing and help with costs.

Why is my baby always sick since starting daycare?

Babies in group care often catch more colds in the first year. Have a backup plan for sick days, and call your baby's doctor with any worries.
