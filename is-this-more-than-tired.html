A quick check-in

Is this more than tired?

Every new dad is tired. But sometimes tired turns into something heavier, and it’s hard to tell from the inside. These questions aren’t a test, and there’s no score. They’re a way to notice what’s going on, and to know when it’s time to talk to someone.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Read the questions below and notice which ones sound like you.
  • If any has lasted two weeks or more, or keeps getting worse, talk to your doctor.
  • Thinking about suicide? Call or text 988 now. In an emergency, call 911.

Questions to ask yourself

Think about the last two weeks.

  • Sleep. Do you lie awake even when the baby is asleep and you finally have the chance to rest? More in can’t sleep even when the baby does.
  • Mood. Do you feel flat, numb or down most days? Have things you used to enjoy stopped feeling good?
  • Anger. Are you snapping at people, or getting angry over small things more than you used to?
  • Pulling away. Are you staying late at work, avoiding home, or going quiet with your partner and friends?
  • Worry. Is your mind racing with worry you can’t switch off?
  • Scary thoughts. Do sudden, unwanted thoughts of something bad happening to the baby pop into your head? They’re common, and having them doesn’t mean you’ll act on them. Read scary thoughts about the baby.
  • Drinking. Are you drinking more, or leaning on something else to get through the day? More in drinking more since the baby.
  • Feeling hopeless. Do you feel like things won’t get better, or that your family would be better off without you? If so, call or text 988 now. You don’t have to be in crisis to call.

What your answers mean

There’s no score here, and only a doctor can tell you what’s going on. But if any of these sound like you, and it has lasted two weeks or more, is getting worse, or gets in the way of work or home, that’s worth taking seriously.

It doesn’t mean you’re weak or a bad dad. Depression and anxiety after a baby happen to dads too, and they’re treatable. Read the signs in dads for more.

Who to talk to

  • Your doctor. Book a visit and say what you’ve noticed, in plain words. How to talk to your doctor as a dad has words you can use.
  • The PSI HelpLine. Call 1-800-944-4773, or text the word Help to the same number. A trained volunteer connects dads with support and local providers. It’s not a crisis line, and not instant: a volunteer replies 8am to 11pm ET.
  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline. Call or text 1-833-852-6262, any time, day or night. Dads too.
  • 988. If you’re thinking about suicide, or about hurting yourself or someone else, call or text 988 now. In an emergency, call 911.

Want a more detailed check?

These free online checks are optional. They can’t diagnose you either, but you can take your answers to your doctor.

  • HeadsUpGuys depression check: nine questions, anonymous, made for men.
  • Postpartum Men: a site for fathers that includes a self-assessment for postpartum depression.

You’re not the only one

Plenty of dads have felt this and come out the other side. Some have shared their stories: see real dads’ stories. On our message board, you can talk with other dads any time, under a username.

Common questions

How do I know if it’s more than being tired?

If low mood, anger, worry, sleeplessness or pulling away has lasted two weeks or more, is getting worse, or gets in the way of daily life, it’s worth talking to a doctor.

Is there a test for postpartum depression in dads?

Doctors use short questionnaires to check for depression and anxiety. This page has no score, only questions to help you notice what’s going on. Your doctor can check properly.

What should I do if I’m thinking about suicide?

Call or text 988 now, any time, day or night. In an emergency, call 911.
