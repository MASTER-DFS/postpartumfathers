When your toddler wanders off

You looked down for a second, and when you looked up, your kid was gone. Your heart drops. It happens to almost every parent of a toddler, and most of the time they’re found within minutes. Here’s what to do right away, and how to make it less likely next time.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Right now

  • Call their name and look low, under racks and inside hanging clothes.
  • Tell a worker right away: age and what they’re wearing.
  • Can’t find them in a few minutes, or there’s water or traffic nearby? Call 911.

The first minute

  1. Call their name loudly, and look low. Little kids hide under racks, behind displays and in the middle of hanging clothes, and they often think it’s a game.
  2. Tell the nearest worker right away. Say “My child is missing” and give their age and what they’re wearing. Many big stores have a lost-child plan, often called Code Adam, where staff watch the doors and start searching.
  3. Say it out loud to the people around you: “I’m looking for a 3-year-old in a blue jacket.” Strangers will help look.
  4. Check the dangerous places first if there are any: the parking lot, a street, a pool, a pond or any water.
  5. Call 911 if you can’t find them in a few minutes, or right away if there’s water, traffic or a crowd nearby. Calling early is never a mistake.

Most of the time, the child turns up within minutes, a few feet away, hiding or looking at something. That’s worth knowing, so you can stay clear-headed. But treat every one as real until they’re back in your hands.

At home, or outdoors

At home, check the places a small child can crawl into and get stuck or fall asleep: closets, under beds, the car, the dryer, a toy chest. If you have a pool, a hot tub or water nearby, check that first, every time. Outdoors, at a park or a beach, tell the lifeguard or staff and call 911 quickly.

Help 911 find them faster

  • Take a photo on your phone before a big outing, a store run or a fair. Then you have exactly what they’re wearing today.
  • Know their height and weight roughly, and any glasses or marks.
  • Tell the police where and when you last saw them and what they had on.

If a child is truly missing, after you’ve called the police, the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children has a 24/7 line: 1-800-843-5678.

Teach them what to do if they’re lost

Practice this at home, like a game, a few times a year. Short and simple works best for a toddler or preschooler.

  • Stop and stay. If you can’t see me, stay where you are. I’ll come find you.
  • Ask a worker or a mom with kids. Point out store workers and their name tags or shirts next time you’re out.
  • Know your name and my name. Not “Daddy,” your real first and last name.
  • A phone number on them. Until they can say it, write your number on a wristband, a sticker inside their shirt, or on their arm in marker for a busy day out.
  • Never hide from me in a store, even as a game. Make it a family rule.

Making it less likely

  • Bright clothes on busy outings are easier to spot.
  • A cart or a hand to hold in big stores and parking lots.
  • Agree who’s watching. When two adults are out, say it out loud: “You’ve got him.” Most kids go missing when each adult thinks the other one has them.
  • Lock up at home. Door knob covers, high latches and a closed gate keep a wanderer in. See baby-proofing your home.

Afterwards, for you

Those seconds can feel like an hour. When they turn up, your legs may shake for a while, and you might replay it for days. That’s your body coming down from a real scare, and it’s normal.

Every parent looks away. Kids are fast, and they slip off from careful, loving parents all the time. Hug them, and save the lesson for later when you’re both calm. Something like “I was scared because I couldn’t see you. Next time, stop and stay.”

If the worry won’t switch off long after, see anxious and on edge.

If it gets dark

If you’re running on empty and things feel too heavy, call or text 988, or the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262. Both are free, 24/7, and dads can call. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

What do I do if my child goes missing in a store?

Call their name and look low, under racks and inside hanging clothes. Tell the nearest worker right away with your child's age and what they're wearing. Many stores have a lost-child plan, often called Code Adam, where staff watch the doors. Call 911 if you can't find them in a few minutes.

When should I call 911 if my toddler wanders off?

Right away if there's water, traffic or a big crowd nearby. Otherwise, if you can't find them within a few minutes of searching. Calling early is never a mistake.

How do I teach my toddler what to do if they get lost?

Keep it short and practice it like a game: stop and stay where you are, ask a worker or a mom with kids for help, and know your real name and your parent's name. Until they can say your phone number, put it on a wristband or a sticker inside their shirt.
