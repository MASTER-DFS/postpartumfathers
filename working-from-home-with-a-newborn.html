Working from home

Working from home with a newborn? Here’s how to get a breather

Working from home sounds like the best of both worlds, and in many ways it is. You're there for the little moments. But a lot of dads find they never really clock out of either job. If you're running from your laptop straight into bath time with no break in between, you're not alone, and a few small changes can help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Why it's so draining

  • You hear every cry through the wall during calls, and part of your brain is always listening.
  • There's no line between work and home. You answer emails at the changing table and change diapers between meetings.
  • The second shift starts the minute work ends. No commute, no quiet drive home, no time to decompress.
  • Nobody at work sees it. On camera you look fine, even on three hours of sleep.
  • It gets lonely. You may go days without talking to another adult outside of work.

What helps

  • Build a fake commute. When work ends, take ten minutes before family time: a walk around the block, a short drive, or sitting in the car with music. It gives your brain the gap a real commute used to.
  • Make a door you can close. Even a corner with headphones helps your brain know when you're working.
  • Noise-cancelling headphones or a white noise app can make a big difference to how drained you feel by the end of the day.
  • Set work hours and stick to them. Agree at home on when you're on the clock and when you're free to help.
  • Block your calendar for the busiest baby times, like a feed or a nap handover, if your job allows.
  • Get out of the house at lunch. Even around the block with the stroller breaks up the day.
  • Close the laptop at the end of the day. Shut it, put it away, and be home.
  • Keep in touch with people. A quick call with a friend or another dad counts. So does our message board.

Many employers also offer an Employee Assistance Program with a few free, confidential counseling sessions. It's worth checking your benefits. More on that in going back to work after the baby.

Meetings with a crying baby in the background

  • Mute by default. Unmute only when you talk.
  • Turn on noise suppression. Teams and Zoom both have a setting that filters out background noise, including crying. Look under audio settings.
  • Use a headset with a close mic. It picks up your voice, not the room.
  • Say it once, up front. "I've got a newborn at home, so you might hear a cry." Most people are kind about it, and plenty of them have been there.
  • Book big calls around the baby's day when you can, like after a feed or during a usual nap.
  • It's okay to step away. "Give me two minutes" is fine. A crying baby is not a sign you're doing your job badly.

The 5pm switch: from work straight to dinner

For a lot of work-from-home dads, the hardest moment is when the laptop closes. There's no drive home, just hungry kids and dinner to make, right now. A little planning earlier in the day makes that switch much easier.

  • Start dinner at lunch. Load the slow cooker or prep a sheet pan at noon, so at 5pm you just turn on the oven.
  • Cook twice on the weekend. Make a double batch on Sunday and save the leftovers for the toughest weeknights.
  • Keep a short rotation. Five or six easy meals you can make without thinking means no deciding at 5pm.
  • Give the kids a job. Washing vegetables, setting the table or stirring keeps them busy and close to you.
  • Take five minutes first. Even a short walk around the block or a minute outside before you start cooking helps you shift gears.
  • Simple counts. Eggs, pasta or a frozen meal is still dinner. Fed and calm beats fancy.

When it's more than a long week

If you feel like you're always on edge, snapping at people, can't sleep, or feel numb for more than two weeks, that can be anxiety or depression, and both are common in new dads. Read worn down as a new dad and how to talk to your doctor. Other dads get it: PSI's free dads' groups meet online, which works from a home office too.

Common questions

How do I work from home with a newborn?

Set clear work hours, have a space with a door or headphones, plan baby handovers around your calendar, get outside at lunch, and take ten minutes to decompress before family time.

Why is working from home with a baby so exhausting?

You hear the baby all day, work and home blur together, and family time starts the minute work ends, with no commute to decompress. It is common, and small boundaries help.

How do I handle meetings with a baby crying in the background?

Mute by default, turn on the noise suppression setting in Teams or Zoom, use a headset with a close mic, and mention once that you have a newborn. Most people understand.

How do I handle dinner right after work when I work from home?

Start dinner at lunch with a slow cooker or sheet pan, cook a double batch on the weekend, keep a short rotation of easy meals, and give the kids small jobs.

How can I decompress when I work from home?

Try a fake commute: a ten-minute walk, drive or quiet sit in the car between closing the laptop and family time.

What if I need help tonight?

Call or text 988 any time, or the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262, which helps dads too. In an emergency, call 911.
