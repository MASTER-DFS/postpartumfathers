Expectations

When fatherhood isn’t what you expected

You pictured what being a dad would feel like. Maybe it was proud moments, a happy house, and knowing what to do. Instead it might feel like exhaustion, noise, and wondering where you went. If that's where you are, you're not alone. Many dads feel this, and very few say it out loud.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Name what you miss. One thing from your old life. Missing it doesn't mean you don't love this one.
  • Notice one good moment from today. It probably didn't look like the picture, and it still counts.
  • Tell one person how it really is. Not the highlight reel. Post it on our message board if that's easier.

It's okay to miss your old life

Sleeping in. Seeing friends without planning it a week ahead. Your hobbies, your quiet, your time. Missing those things is normal. It doesn't mean you regret your child. It means your life changed fast, and part of you hasn't caught up yet.

When you feel like you've lost yourself

For a while, your days can be all about everyone else. Work, the baby, the house, the next thing that needs doing. It's easy to feel like the person you were is gone.

He isn't gone. He's on hold. The things that made you, you are still there, and you can bring pieces of them back, a little at a time.

When nothing feels in your control

Babies don't keep schedules. Plans fall through. You can do everything right and the night still goes sideways. For a dad who is used to fixing things, that can be really hard. Feeling out of control isn't a sign you're doing it wrong. It's what this stage is like for almost everyone.

This is grief, not failure

When life doesn't match what you pictured, it's normal to grieve. You can love your kids completely and still feel sad about what changed. Both are true at the same time.

Grief that nobody names tends to come out sideways, often as a short fuse or pulling away. If you see that in yourself, the signs in dads are worth a read.

Small steps that help

  • Let go of the picture. The real version will have its own good moments. They just might not look like what you imagined.
  • Keep one thread to who you were. A song, a team, a skill, a friend. You don't need hours, just a connection.
  • Talk more than you fix. What helps most is usually someone who listens, not someone with advice.
  • Talk to other dads. Hearing "me too" from someone who's been there can change how you feel.

Where to talk

  • Our message board, open any time.
  • PSI's free dads' groups, which meet online.
  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, free and 24/7, dads too.
  • 988, call or text, if you're struggling or having thoughts of not wanting to be here.

If you're worried you're failing, read feel like you're failing as a dad. If the heaviness hasn't lifted in a couple of weeks, talking to your doctor is a good next step. Feeling better usually starts with telling someone.

Common questions

Is it normal to miss your old life after having a baby?

Yes. Missing sleep, free time, friends and hobbies is normal. It doesn't mean you regret your child. Your life changed fast, and it takes time to adjust.

Why do I feel like I've lost myself as a dad?

Early parenthood can make your days all about everyone else. The person you were is on hold, not gone. Keeping small threads to your old life helps.

Is it okay to feel disappointed in fatherhood?

Yes. When life doesn't match what you pictured, it's normal to grieve. You can love your kids and still feel sad about what changed. It's grief, not failure.

What helps when fatherhood feels overwhelming?

Talk to someone, especially other dads. Keep one thread to who you were, and if the heaviness lasts more than a couple of weeks, talk to a doctor.
