When the baby gets hurt

When the baby falls off the bed

You looked away for a second, and the baby rolled off the bed, the couch or the changing table. Your stomach drops. Almost every parent goes through this at least once, and most babies are fine. Here’s what to check, when to call, and how to make it less likely next time.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Right now

  • Check the baby first. Crying right away is a good sign.
  • Call 911 or go to the ER if the baby is hard to wake, throws up more than once, won’t move an arm or leg, or anything looks wrong.
  • Seems fine? Under two and hit their head: call the pediatrician today, and watch them for the next day.

First, check the baby

Go to the baby and look them over. If they’re crying, that’s a good sign. It’s fine to pick them up and comfort them. If they aren’t moving or won’t wake, call 911 and leave them where they are unless they’re in danger. Most falls from a bed, couch or changing table end with a startled baby who settles in a few minutes.

Call 911 or go to the ER right away if the baby:

  • Is hard to wake, or seems very sleepy or floppy
  • Passed out, even for a moment, or has a seizure
  • Throws up more than once
  • Won’t move an arm or leg, or screams when you touch it
  • Can’t be settled no matter what you try, or won’t feed
  • Has one pupil bigger than the other
  • Has a bulging soft spot, a dent, or a big squishy swelling on the head
  • Has blood or clear fluid coming from the nose or ears
  • Just doesn’t seem like themselves, or anything looks wrong to you

If the baby seems fine

A baby who cries right away, calms down, and goes back to feeding, looking around and acting like themselves is usually okay. A small bump or red mark is common.

  • Call the pediatrician the same day if the baby is under two and hit their head, even if they seem fine. Most offices have a nurse line or an on-call doctor after hours.
  • Keep a close eye on them for the next day. Some signs show up hours later. Watch for anything on the list above.
  • Naps are okay. You don’t need to keep a baby awake after a bump. Check that they wake the way they usually do.
  • A cold cloth on a bump for a few minutes can help, if the baby will let you.

You are not overreacting by calling. It’s a short call that either puts your mind at rest or catches something early.

How to stop it happening again

Babies can wiggle, push off and roll sooner than anyone expects, sometimes the very first time on the day it happens. That’s why almost every parent has a story like this.

  • Keep one hand on the baby any time they’re up on a bed, couch or changing table. Even while you reach for a wipe.
  • Get everything ready first. Diaper, wipes and clothes within reach before you lay the baby down.
  • Change them on the floor when you’re tired or in a hurry. A towel or mat on the floor can’t be fallen off.
  • Use the changing table strap, but don’t count on it. Your hand is the real safety.
  • If you need to step away, even for a second, put the baby in the crib or on the floor, or take them with you.
  • Never set a car seat or bouncer on a bed, table or counter. They can tip and fall. Floor only.
  • Don’t leave the baby to sleep on an adult bed or couch. Falls are one reason, and there are more in safe sleep and the fear of SIDS.

The guilt afterwards

A lot of dads replay it for days. “I only turned around for a second.” Every parent turns around. Falls happen in careful, loving homes all the time, and to whoever happens to be at the changing table, so if it was your partner, the same goes for them.

What matters is what you did next: you checked, you watched, and now you know a little more. That’s what a good dad does.

If the worry won’t switch off, and you keep checking the baby or can’t settle long after they’re fine, that counts too. See anxious and on edge.

If it gets dark

If you’re running on empty and things feel too heavy, call or text 988, or the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262. Both are free, 24/7, and dads can call. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

My baby fell off the bed. When should I go to the ER?

Call 911 or go to the ER right away if the baby is hard to wake or very sleepy, passed out, has a seizure, throws up more than once, won't move an arm or leg, can't be settled, won't feed, has unequal pupils, a bulging soft spot, a dent or big swelling on the head, blood or clear fluid from the nose or ears, or just doesn't seem like themselves.

My baby fell and seems fine. What should I do?

If the baby cried, calmed down and is acting like themselves, that is usually a good sign. If they hit their head and are under two, call the pediatrician the same day anyway. Keep a close eye on them for the next day, because some signs show up later.

Can my baby sleep after falling off the bed?

Yes. You don't need to keep a baby awake after a bump. Check that they wake the way they usually do, and call the doctor or go in if they are hard to wake or anything seems off.

How do I stop my baby from rolling off the changing table?

Keep one hand on the baby the whole time, get everything ready before you lay them down, and change them on the floor when you are tired. Use the strap but don't rely on it. If you need to step away, take the baby with you or put them in the crib.
