The baby

Worried your baby is behind

Not rolling yet. Not babbling like your buddy’s kid. It’s easy to worry. Most of the time babies are just on their own schedule. Here’s what’s normal, and what to do if your gut says ask.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Stop comparing to other babies. A range is normal.
  • Write down what you’ve noticed, and take a short video if you can.
  • Bring it to the doctor. Asking is never a waste of their time.

Every baby has their own pace

Babies don’t read the charts. Some roll early and talk late. Some do the opposite. Comparing to your friend’s baby, or a video online, can scare you for no reason. A range is normal.

But your gut matters too. You see your baby every day. If something feels off, it’s always OK to ask.

What doctors look for

Milestones fall into a few areas: moving (holding the head up, rolling, sitting), talking and sounds, social (smiling, looking at faces), and learning (reaching, exploring). The CDC’s free Milestone Tracker app shows what most babies do at each age, with photos and videos.

If your baby was born early, ask the doctor whether to count from the due date instead of the birth date. Many preemies catch up in their own time.

If you’re worried

  • Write down what you’ve noticed, with examples and a rough date. Short videos on your phone help a lot.
  • Bring it up at the next checkup, or call sooner if it’s bugging you. You’re not being a pain. That’s what the doctor is there for.
  • Ask directly: “Is this something we should watch, or check now?”
  • Losing a skill they used to have is always worth a call to the doctor.

Early intervention is free to ask for

Every state has an early intervention program for babies and toddlers under 3. The CDC says you can ask for a free evaluation yourself. You don’t need a doctor’s referral or a diagnosis. Find your state’s program.

Getting checked doesn’t mean something is wrong. And if your baby does need some help, starting early makes a real difference. The CDC puts it simply: don’t wait.

Things you can do every day

  • Talk to your baby all the time. Narrate what you’re doing. It builds language.
  • Tummy time while they’re awake and you’re watching.
  • Read, sing, make faces. Copy their sounds back.
  • More ideas in things to do with your baby.

When the worry takes over

If you’re searching symptoms every night or can’t stop the what-ifs, that’s anxiety, and it’s common in new dads. Read anxious and on edge. If your baby has a diagnosis, see when your baby has a health condition.

If it gets heavy

If the worry is wearing you down, call or text the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262, free and 24/7, dads too. If you’re thinking about suicide, call or text 988. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

How do I know if my baby is behind?

Babies develop at their own pace, so a range is normal. The CDC's free Milestone Tracker app shows what most babies do at each age. If something feels off, write down what you notice and ask your baby's doctor.

Do I need a referral for early intervention?

No. The CDC says you can ask your state's early intervention program for a free evaluation yourself, for babies and toddlers under 3, without a referral or diagnosis.

Should I count my premature baby's age from the due date?

Ask your baby's doctor. Many doctors use the age from the due date for preemies for a while.
