Getting help

How to talk to your doctor as a new dad

A lot of dads never bring it up because they don't know what to say, or worry it'll sound like a big deal. Doctors hear this all the time, and it's one of the best steps you can take. You don't need the right words. Here are some you can borrow.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

What you can say

"Since the baby was born, I haven't been feeling like myself. Can we talk about it?"

That's enough. You don't have to say "depression" or "anxiety," or name anything at all. Your doctor will know what to ask. If it helps, add what you've noticed, like:

  • "I'm irritable all the time and snapping at people."
  • "I can't sleep even when the baby does."
  • "I can't stop worrying."
  • "I feel numb, or like I don't enjoy anything."
  • "I'm stressed and running on empty."
  • "I'm just not myself lately."

If it's hard to say face to face, write it on your phone and show it, or put it in the message when you book. Some dads find it easier to start with a text: you can text the word Help to the PSI HelpLine at 1-800-944-4773 for support and referrals. A volunteer writes back, but not right away. It's not a crisis line.

What to expect

  • A few questions about your mood, sleep, and how long it's been going on. Sometimes it's a short questionnaire.
  • A conversation about options. That might be talk therapy, medication, or both. You get a say in what you try.
  • A check on your physical health, since things like thyroid problems can affect mood.
  • A follow-up to see how you're doing.

Doctors talk with new parents about mood every day. Bringing it up doesn't mean anyone will think you're a bad dad. It means you're looking after your family by looking after yourself.

Tips that help

  • Book a visit just for this, so it doesn't get squeezed into the last minute of something else.
  • Write down what you've noticed and how long it's been going on.
  • Be honest about drinking, sleep, and any dark thoughts. It helps them help you.
  • Bring someone if you'd like the support.
  • If you don't feel heard, it's fine to ask for a referral or see someone else.

No doctor?

You can still get help. The PSI directory lists therapists who work with new parents, and FindTreatment.gov is the free government finder for mental health care near you. Many clinics offer video visits too. Costs vary, so ask about insurance or a sliding scale.

Common questions

What should I say to my doctor about postpartum depression as a dad?

You can simply say: since the baby was born, I haven't been feeling like myself, can we talk about it? You don't need to use the word depression. Your doctor will know what to ask.

Can dads be screened for postpartum depression?

Yes. A doctor can ask a few questions or use a short questionnaire to check for depression or anxiety in fathers.

Where can a dad get help without a doctor?

The PSI directory lists therapists who work with new parents, and FindTreatment.gov is a free government finder for mental health care. You can also call the PSI HelpLine at 1-800-944-4773.
