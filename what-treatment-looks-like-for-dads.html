Getting help

What getting help actually looks like

A lot of dads put off help because they picture the worst: being judged, being labeled, pills that change who they are. The real thing is usually much more ordinary. Here's what actually happens.

If you're ever thinking about suicide or feel your family would be better off without you, please call or text 988 any time, day or night. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911. More crisis help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • You don't have to decide everything. The first step is one conversation.
  • Book a visit with your own doctor, in person or by video, and say "I haven't felt like myself since the baby."
  • Write down three things you've noticed, so you don't have to find the words on the spot.

The first visit

For most dads the first step is their own doctor, in person or by video. It's usually a normal appointment, often 15 to 30 minutes.

  • They'll ask questions about your sleep, appetite, mood, energy, and how long you've felt this way. You may fill in a short questionnaire.
  • They may order a blood test to rule out things like thyroid problems, which can cause similar feelings.
  • You don't need the right words. "I haven't felt like myself since the baby" is enough to start.
  • You leave with a next step. That might be a referral to a therapist, a follow-up visit, medicine, or a mix.

Not sure how to bring it up? See how to talk to your doctor.

What therapy is like

Therapy is talking with a trained counselor, usually once a week for about 45 to 60 minutes. Lots of people do it by video, from home or a parked car.

The first session is mostly them getting to know you: what's going on, how long, and what you'd like to be different. After that, it's practical. Two of the best-studied kinds for depression are:

  • Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT). You learn to spot thoughts and habits that drag you down, and try small changes between sessions.
  • Interpersonal therapy (IPT). It focuses on your relationships and big life changes, like becoming a dad, and on getting the support you need.

Many people go for a few months, not years. It's fine to switch if the first therapist doesn't feel right. Fit matters more than anything. See finding a therapist, and paying for it.

What medicine is like

Your doctor may suggest an antidepressant, on its own or alongside therapy. A few plain facts:

  • It takes time. Antidepressants usually take 4 to 8 weeks to fully work. Sleep, appetite and focus often get better before your mood does.
  • Side effects are worth mentioning. If something bothers you, tell your doctor. There are different medicines and doses to try.
  • Don't stop on your own. Talk to your doctor before you stop or change anything, even if you feel better.
  • If you're under 25, watch closely in the first few weeks. Some young people have more dark thoughts at first. If that happens, tell your doctor and call or text 988.
  • It's a decision you make together. You and your doctor decide whether to try it, and for how long.

Taking medicine for depression isn't weakness, any more than taking medicine for blood pressure. The goal is to feel like yourself again.

How long until you feel better

It's rarely overnight. Most dads notice small things first: sleeping a little better, less snapping, a bit more patience. There will be good weeks and bad weeks, and that's normal. Depression is very treatable, and most people do get better with help. If nothing has changed after a couple of months, say so. Changing the plan is part of the process, not a failure.

Will anyone find out?

  • Your health records are private. Your employer isn't told unless you choose to tell them. Employee Assistance Programs are confidential too.
  • Therapists keep what you say private. The main exception is if someone's safety is at real risk.
  • Saying you're struggling isn't the same as being a danger. Doctors hear about anger, numbness and unwanted scary thoughts all the time. Their job is to help you get better. See scary thoughts about the baby.
  • If you own guns and worry about that coming up, see firearms and a new baby.

What to say

  • "Since the baby came I haven't felt like myself. I'm angry or flat most days, and it's been a few weeks."
  • "I'd like to try talking to someone. Can you refer me?"
  • "I'm nervous about medicine. Can we talk through what it would be like?"

Worried about cost? See getting help without insurance. Not sure you need it? Try is this more than tired?

Common questions

What happens at the first doctor visit for depression?

The doctor asks about your sleep, appetite, mood and how long it has lasted, may give you a short questionnaire or a blood test, and agrees a next step with you, like therapy, medicine or a follow-up.

How long does therapy for depression take?

Usually weekly sessions of about 45 to 60 minutes. Many people go for a few months, not years, and many do it by video.

How long do antidepressants take to work?

Usually 4 to 8 weeks to fully work. Sleep, appetite and focus often improve before mood. Talk to your doctor before stopping or changing them.

Will my employer find out if I get treatment?

Not unless you tell them. Your health records are private, and Employee Assistance Programs are confidential.
