Work and family

When your partner goes back to work after the baby

The end of her leave is a big change for the whole house. There's a new morning rush, a new plan for the baby, and a lot of feelings on her side and yours. A little planning before the first day makes it much easier.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Write down who does what on a normal weekday, from wake-up to bedtime.
  • Decide now who stays home when the baby gets sick.
  • Do a practice run of the morning a few days before her first day.

Share the load out loud

When one parent has been home, they've often been carrying most of the house without anyone noticing. Once you're both working, that has to change. Sit down together and list the daily jobs: drop-off, pick-up, bottles, dinner, bath, night wake-ups, laundry. Split them out loud, so nothing is assumed. There's more in carrying the house in your head.

Mornings and the first weeks

  • Pack the night before: bags, bottles, clothes, keys.
  • Split the morning: one of you does the baby, the other does everything else.
  • Keep the first week light. No big plans, easy dinners, early nights.
  • Expect a rough start. Babies often get sick in the first weeks of new care. It does settle down.

If the baby is starting daycare, read starting daycare. If you're the one staying home now, see stay-at-home dads.

Sick days and backup

A sick baby can't go to daycare, and it will happen at the worst time. Agree now on how you'll take turns staying home, and line up one or two backup people who can step in. Check what leave each of your jobs offers for a sick child.

If she's pumping at work

Under the federal PUMP Act, most employees can take reasonable break time to pump, in a private space that isn't a bathroom, for up to a year after the baby is born. You can help by washing pump parts, packing the bag the night before, and taking care of the bottles at home. If you work, check your own job's leave and flexibility too in going back to work.

The feelings that come with it

She may feel guilty about leaving the baby, relieved to be back, or both on the same day. All of it is normal. Don't judge either feeling. Ask how she's doing after the first few days, and mean it.

A return to work can also stir up worry or low mood. If she seems anxious or not herself for more than a couple of weeks, see helping your partner with postpartum anxiety or helping your partner.

Keep something for the two of you

With two jobs and a baby, the first thing to go is time together. Even ten minutes after the baby goes down, phones away, helps. So does saying thank you for the small things.

And keep an eye on yourself. If you're running on empty, read work stress and a new baby.

Common questions

How do we split chores when we both go back to work?

Sit down together and list the daily jobs, from drop-off and bottles to dinner, bath and night wake-ups, and split them out loud so nothing is assumed.

Who stays home when the baby is sick?

Agree before her first day on how you'll take turns, line up one or two backup people, and check what leave each of your jobs offers for a sick child.

Does she get breaks to pump at work?

Under the federal PUMP Act, most employees can take reasonable break time to pump, in a private space that isn't a bathroom, for up to a year after the baby is born.

Is it normal for her to feel guilty or relieved going back to work?

Yes. Many moms feel guilty, relieved, or both. If she seems anxious or not herself for more than a couple of weeks, help her talk to her doctor.
