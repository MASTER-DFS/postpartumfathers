Feeling shut in

When home starts to feel like a prison

You work in the house, parent in the house, and try to rest in the house. Some weeks you barely leave. The walls start to close in, and every day blurs into the next. Many dads know this feeling, and it's more common than people say. It doesn't mean you don't love your family. It means you need a change of scenery.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Step outside tomorrow morning. Five minutes of daylight on the porch or in the yard, coffee in hand.
  • Turn one errand into an outing. Take the kids along and make it the trip out of the house.
  • Pick one thing outside the house this week that's just for you, and put it on the calendar.

Why being stuck inside wears you down

  • Nothing changes. Same rooms, same view, same noise. Your brain stops getting a fresh start.
  • Little daylight. Days spent indoors can leave you sluggish and low, and can throw off your sleep.
  • Days run together. Without anywhere to go, Tuesday looks like Saturday.

Cabin fever is real. And with kids at home, it's easy to stop noticing how long it's been since you were somewhere else.

Leave the house, kids and all

You don't need a free afternoon to leave the house. Getting out with the kids counts too:

  • The yard or the porch. Eat breakfast or a snack outside when the weather allows.
  • A drive while the baby sleeps. Car seat buckled, music on, nowhere you have to be.
  • Errands as outings. The hardware store, the grocery store, picking up takeout. Kids often love it.
  • Free places nearby. A park, the library, a walking trail. Many libraries run free story times for little kids.

Even ten minutes of fresh air and daylight can change how the rest of the day feels.

Change the scenery inside

Some days you really can't get out. Small changes still help. Let the light in first thing. Work from a different spot for an afternoon. Move playtime to a different room. Put on music that isn't for the kids.

If work and home have no edge between them, working from home with a newborn covers the 5pm switch and how a short "fake commute" helps.

One place each week that's just yours

Pick one regular thing that gets you out of the house on your own. A gym class, a run, a haircut, coffee with a friend, a dads' meetup. Keep it small and put it on the calendar, so it actually happens.

For how to ask for that time, read no time to yourself. If you're missing other adults, read lonely as a new dad. And if your whole week feels like it has no break anywhere, see going from one kid to two.

When trapped turns into hopeless

Feeling stuck now and then is normal. It's time to talk to someone if you dread every day, feel hopeless about anything changing, or keep thinking about escaping or disappearing.

  • Our message board, open any time.
  • PSI's free dads' groups, which meet online.
  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, free and 24/7, dads too.
  • 988, call or text, if you're having thoughts of not wanting to be here.

This stage is hard, and it does change as the kids grow.

Common questions

Why does home feel like a prison with a new baby?

When you work, parent and rest in the same place, with little daylight and few other adults, days blur together. It's a common feeling and doesn't mean you don't love your family.

How can I get out of the house with a baby and kids?

Outings with the kids count. Try the yard or porch, a drive while the baby sleeps, errands as outings, or free places like a park or the library.

What if I can't leave the house at all?

Change the scenery inside. Open blinds and windows, work from a different spot, move playtime to another room, or put on your own music.

When is feeling trapped a sign to get help?

If you dread every day, feel hopeless, or keep thinking about escaping or disappearing, talk to someone. Call or text 988 if you have thoughts of not wanting to be here.
