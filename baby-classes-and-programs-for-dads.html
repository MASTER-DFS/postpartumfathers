Classes and programs

Baby classes and family programs for dads

Look for baby classes and almost everything is called "mommy and me." It can seem like nothing out there is for dads. There's more than it looks like. Most of those classes welcome any parent, and plenty of programs are free.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Look up your library's baby story time. It's free, and any parent can go.
  • Ask "are dads welcome?" before you skip a "mommy and me" class. The answer is usually yes.
  • Call 211 and ask about parent and baby programs near you.

"Mommy and me" usually means any parent

Many classes use that name out of habit. A quick call or message asking whether fathers can join usually gets a yes.

You might be the only dad at first. That's okay. Other dads often start coming once they see one there.

Library programs

  • Library baby story time. Babies get songs, rhymes and bouncing, and you get a room of other parents. Free at most public libraries. Look for "baby" or "lapsit" on the events calendar. Many libraries also have open play times, where little ones play with the library's toys and you meet other families.
  • "1,000 Books Before Kindergarten." Many libraries run this free reading challenge, starting from birth. Every book you read counts, and kids get small prizes along the way.

Family programs in your county

Many counties have family resource centers or early childhood programs for families with kids under five. They often run parent and child classes, playgroups and family events, and many are free or low cost. Three ways to find yours:

  • Help Me Grow. A national network with programs in many states that connects families with young kids to local programs and support. Find your state on their list.
  • Early Head Start. Free programs for babies, toddlers and pregnant moms, for families who qualify, often based on income. Use the Head Start locator to find one near you.
  • 211. Call 211 and ask about parent and baby programs in your area. It's free and confidential.

In California, every county also has a First 5 office that runs programs for families with kids under five.

Paid classes worth a look

These cost money. Ask about prices and whether you can try one class first.

  • Parent and baby swim classes, usually from about 6 months. More in bonding with your baby.
  • Baby music classes. Singing, shakers and bouncing. Babies don't care if you can sing.
  • Parks and recreation classes. Most cities and counties put out a seasonal activity guide with baby and toddler classes, like music, movement and water play. Search for your city's parks and recreation activity guide.
  • The YMCA. Many Ys offer parent and child classes and family swim times. Many also offer financial help with fees, so ask at the front desk.

Find other dads

  • Dads Supporting Dads has local dad groups in some cities, with family play dates and dads' nights out, plus groups online. See if there's one near you.
  • Boot Camp for New Dads is a workshop for dads-to-be, taught by recent dads who bring their babies. Once yours is here, you can go back as one of those dads, baby in tow. There's usually a fee.

Common questions

Are mommy and me classes open to dads?

Usually, yes. Many classes use that name out of habit. A quick call to check usually gets a yes.

Are there free programs for families with babies?

Many libraries run free baby story times and reading challenges. Help Me Grow, Early Head Start and 211 can point you to local family programs, many free or low cost.

Are there groups for dads with babies?

Yes. Dads Supporting Dads has local groups in some cities and online, and Boot Camp for New Dads lets recent dads come back with their babies.

Do baby classes cost money?

Swim, music, YMCA and parks and recreation classes usually do. Ask about prices and financial help. Many YMCAs offer it.
