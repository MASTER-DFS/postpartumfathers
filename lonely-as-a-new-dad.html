Feeling lonely as a new dad? Here’s how to find your people

Your days are full, but somehow you've never felt more alone. Friends drift, there's no time to see anyone, and it feels like nobody asks how you're doing. A lot of new dads feel this way and never say it. The good news is it doesn't take much to start feeling connected again.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Send one text. "Hey, been a while. How are you?" Most friends are glad to hear from you.
  • Talk to dads online. Our message board is open any time, under a username.
  • Bring the baby along. A walk or a coffee with a friend, baby in a carrier, counts.

Why it happens

  • Your time disappears. Nights out, sports and hobbies get squeezed out.
  • Friends without kids may not get it, or stop asking.
  • Men are often less likely to reach out first, so the silence grows.
  • The attention goes to the baby and mom, and dads end up on the outside.

Small ways to reconnect

  • Send one text today. "Hey, been a while. How are you?" Most friends are glad to hear from you.
  • Bring the baby along. A walk, a coffee, or watching a game at a friend's house with the baby in a carrier counts.
  • Find other new dads. Boot Camp for New Dads classes and local parent groups are full of dads in the same spot.
  • Keep one regular thing. A weekly call, a pickup game every other week. Something on the calendar.
  • Talk to dads online. Our message board is open any time, under a username, and PSI's free dads' groups meet online every week.

How to actually start with another dad

Most dads are waiting for someone else to go first. It can be you.

  • Start where you already are. Daycare pickup, the park, swim lessons, a birthday party. “How old is yours?” is enough to open a conversation.
  • Make one plan with a time. “We’re at the park Saturday at ten if you want to come” works better than “we should hang out sometime.”
  • Follow up within a week. A quick text keeps it from fading.
  • Keep it easy. A walk with strollers or coffee counts. It doesn’t need to be a big night out.

It can feel awkward at first. Most dads are glad someone asked.

When loneliness turns into more

If you feel cut off, numb, or hopeless for more than two weeks, it can be a sign of depression, which is common in new dads and very treatable. Read the signs of postpartum depression in dads and how to talk to your doctor.

Common questions

Is it normal to feel lonely as a new dad?

Yes. Many new fathers feel isolated as time with friends shrinks and attention goes to the baby. It is common and it can change.

How can new dads make friends?

Reach out to old friends, bring the baby along to see people, join dad classes or parent groups, and try online dads' groups.

Can loneliness be a sign of depression in dads?

It can. If loneliness comes with numbness or hopelessness for more than two weeks, talk to a doctor.
