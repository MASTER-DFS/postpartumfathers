Traveling for work with a new baby at home

Packing a suitcase when there's a newborn at home is hard. You might feel guilty for leaving, worried about how things will go, and secretly glad for one full night of sleep, all at once. That's normal. With some planning, trips can go smoothly for everyone, and you can stay close while you're away.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Before you go

  • Ask if the trip can wait or shrink. Many managers will push back a trip, send someone else, or make it a video call during the early weeks, if you ask.
  • Keep trips short when you can. One night away is very different from five.
  • Line up help at home. A family member, a friend, or a sitter for the hardest hours, especially evenings and nights.
  • Stock the house. Groceries, diapers, wipes, and a few easy meals in the freezer.
  • Write down the essentials: the baby's doctor, pharmacy, and who to call if something comes up.

When tension builds right before you leave

For a lot of families, the hardest part of a trip is the day or two before it. Everyone is tired, the person staying home is facing days of doing it alone, and you're juggling packing, work and guilt. Short tempers and hard conversations right before a trip are common. It doesn't mean something is wrong at home. It usually means everyone is stretched thin.

  • Talk about the trip early, not the night before, so there's time to plan together.
  • Know that help with tasks isn't the whole answer. A relative coming to stay can cover meals and nights, and still not take away the feeling of being left with it all. That feeling is real, even when the plan is good.
  • Ask what would make it easier, and listen, instead of defending the trip.
  • Keep the last evening calm. Pack earlier, put the laptop away, and spend it at home.
  • Say thank you before you go, and again when you get back.

While you're away

  • Pick a set time to call. A video call at the same time each day gives everyone something to count on.
  • Record yourself reading a short book or singing a song so the baby can hear your voice.
  • Check in, don't take over. Ask how things are going and what would help, rather than managing from a hotel room.
  • Use the sleep. If you get a quiet night, take it. Coming home rested helps everyone.

When you get home

  • Jump back in. Take the next night feed, the bath, or the morning shift.
  • Expect a little catch-up. The baby may have changed even in a few days. Give yourself time to find the rhythm again.

Looking after yourself

Travel on top of a newborn can wear you down. If you're feeling numb, on edge or low for more than two weeks, read worn down as a new dad and how to talk to your doctor. Missing home is real too. PSI's free dads' groups meet online, so you can join from a hotel room.

Common questions

Should I travel for work with a newborn at home?

If you can, ask to delay, shorten or replace trips in the early weeks. When travel can't be avoided, plan help at home and keep trips short.

Why is there so much tension right before a work trip?

It's common. Everyone is tired and the person staying home faces days alone. Talk about the trip early, ask what would help, and keep the last evening calm.

How can dads stay connected while traveling?

Set a regular video call time, record yourself reading or singing for the baby, and check in on how things are going at home.

How do I help at home before a work trip?

Line up help for evenings and nights, stock groceries and baby supplies, freeze a few easy meals, and leave key phone numbers.
