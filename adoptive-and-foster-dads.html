Adoptive and foster dads

You may have waited years. You did the home study, the classes and the paperwork. Then the child arrives, and it's harder than anyone said. Or you feel flat when you expected joy. That happens to plenty of adoptive and foster dads, and it's no sign of a flaw in you. It's a huge change, and you're allowed to find it hard.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Tell your caseworker or agency how it's really going. Hard weeks are expected, and they can often help.
  • Keep days quiet and predictable. Same meals, same bedtime, fewer visitors for now.
  • Name one person you can be honest with, someone who won't just say "you must be so happy".

Yes, dads can struggle after adoption

A low stretch after a child comes home is common enough that adoption agencies talk about it. It's sometimes called post-adoption depression, and it can hit fathers too. You don't have to have given birth to feel the weight of a new child.

In dads it often looks less like sadness and more like:

  • Withdrawing from home life, or staying late at work
  • Snapping over small things
  • Drinking more than you used to
  • Guilt that you're not as happy as you "should" be

When the bond takes a while

Your child may come with a history you weren't part of. An older child may test you, keep their distance, or cling hard. A baby may not settle for you right away. None of that means it won't work.

Trust grows from the same small things repeated: showing up, keeping promises, staying calm when they push. Bonding often comes slowly, and slow is fine.

The parts that are unique to fostering

Foster dads carry something other parents don't. You're asked to love a child fully while knowing they may leave. When a placement ends, the grief is real, even if everyone around you treats it like a job finished.

Visits with birth family, court dates and changing plans can keep the whole house on edge. Give yourself room to feel it. Your agency or a local foster parent group has people who know exactly what this is like.

Where to find support

  • Your agency. Ask what post-adoption or foster support it offers. Many have groups, trainings or a support worker.
  • Other adoptive and foster parents. They get it without you having to explain.
  • PSI's free dads' groups, which meet online.
  • Our message board, open any time.
  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, free and 24/7, dads too.

If the low feeling lasts more than two weeks, read depression in new dads. It applies to you too.

Common questions

Can adoptive dads get depressed after a child comes home?

Yes. A low stretch after adoption is common enough to have a name, post-adoption depression, and it can affect fathers. It's a real reaction to a big change, not a sign you made a mistake.

What if I don't feel bonded to my adopted or foster child yet?

That's common, especially if your child has been through a lot. Trust is built through small things repeated: showing up, keeping promises and staying calm. It often takes time.

How do foster dads cope when a placement ends?

Let yourself grieve. Losing a child from your home is a real loss. Talk with your agency, other foster parents, or a counselor who understands foster care.

Where can adoptive and foster dads get support?

Start with your agency's post-adoption or foster support. Other adoptive and foster parents, PSI's free online dads' groups, and the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262 can also help.
