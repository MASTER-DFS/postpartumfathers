Work stress and a new baby

Big things at work rarely wait for a good time. A new manager, a reorganization, a promotion you've been working toward, all landing while you're running on broken sleep. It can feel like you're giving everything and it's still not enough at work or at home. You're not failing. You're carrying two big jobs at once. Here's how to get through it.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Be honest about what you've got

Running on little sleep makes focus, patience and memory harder for anyone. That doesn't mean you can't do your job well. It means you need to spend your energy on purpose instead of trying to do everything at full speed.

Focus where it counts

  • Pick your top three. Each week, decide the three things that matter most at work, and do those first and well.
  • Use your best hours. If you're sharpest in the morning, guard that time for the hard work.
  • Write things down. A tired brain drops details. Notes and reminders catch them.
  • Let small things be good enough. Not every email needs to be perfect.

With a new boss

  • Ask what success looks like to them in the first few months, so you're aiming at the right things.
  • Share your wins briefly and often. New managers don't know your track record yet.
  • You don't have to explain your home life, but a short "we have a new baby at home" can help someone understand your schedule.

When a promotion is on the line

It's tempting to go all out and hope home holds together. A steadier plan usually works better: do excellent work on the things that matter, be clear about what you want, and ask what it takes to get there. Burning out won't help your case. Showing up steady, week after week, will.

Support you may already have

  • Your employer's benefits. Many workplaces offer an employee assistance program (EAP) with free, confidential counseling. Check with HR or your benefits site.
  • Flexible hours. Ask whether start times, a remote day, or blocking time on your calendar is possible.
  • Leave you haven't used. See taking paternity leave.

Leave work at work, a little

When the workday ends, give yourself a small line between work and home: a short walk, a change of clothes, or five minutes in the car. More ideas are in working from home with a newborn, going back to work after the baby, and no time to yourself.

When it's too much

If you're feeling numb, on edge, or low most days for more than two weeks, or you can't switch off at night, read worn down as a new dad and how to talk to your doctor. Other dads get it too: PSI's free dads' groups meet online.

Common questions

How do I handle work stress with a new baby?

Pick your top three priorities each week, use your sharpest hours for the hard work, write things down, and let small things be good enough.

Should I tell my boss about my new baby?

You don't have to explain your home life, but a short mention can help a manager understand your schedule. It's your call.

Can I go for a promotion with a newborn at home?

Yes. Steady, excellent work on what matters usually beats going all out and burning out. Ask clearly what it takes to get there.

Where can I get support for work stress?

Many employers offer an employee assistance program with free, confidential counseling. Check with HR. If you've felt low most days for more than two weeks, talk to a doctor.
