When the baby gets hurt

Baby-proofing your home

Once a baby starts rolling, scooting and crawling, the whole house looks different. You don’t need every gadget in the store. A few big steps prevent most of the serious accidents, and the rest can come a room at a time.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Anchor the furniture. Dressers, bookcases and TVs, screwed to the wall.
  • Lock up medicine and cleaners, and save Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222.
  • Gate the stairs, and never leave the baby alone in water.

Start on your knees

Before you buy anything, get down on the floor and crawl through each room. You’ll see what the baby sees: the cord hanging off the table, the coin under the couch, the cabinet with the cleaner in it. Do it again every few months, because a baby who couldn’t reach something last month can reach it now.

Start before the baby crawls, usually somewhere around 6 months. Babies always move sooner than you expect.

The kitchen

  • Lock the cabinets under the sink, and any others with cleaners, dishwasher pods, or sharp things. Better still, move those up high.
  • Cook on the back burners and turn pot handles in toward the wall. Stove knob covers help if your knobs are on the front.
  • Keep hot drinks away from the edge, and never hold the baby while you drink something hot or cook.
  • Watch dangling cords, from a coffee maker, kettle or toaster. A baby can pull the whole thing down.
  • Keep the trash out of reach or use a can with a lid that locks.

Medicine, cleaners and other poisons

  • All medicine up high and locked, including vitamins and anything in a purse, backpack or coat pocket. Child-resistant caps slow kids down, they don’t stop them.
  • Laundry pods, cleaners and bug spray stay up high in their own containers.
  • Alcohol, vape liquid and weed edibles are locked away, and leftover drinks get poured out right away.
  • Some houseplants are poisonous. Keep them out of reach or ask Poison Help which ones to avoid.
  • Save Poison Help in your phone: 1-800-222-1222. It’s free, 24/7, and they’ll tell you whether you need the ER.

Stairs, doors and gates

  • Gates at the top and bottom of the stairs once the baby starts crawling. At the top, use a gate screwed into the wall, not a pressure gate that can pop loose.
  • Skip the old accordion gates. They can trap an arm or a neck.
  • Door knob covers or high latches on doors to the outside, the garage, the basement, and the bathroom.
  • Check the outside gates are latched every time, especially to a pool, a street or a driveway.
  • Watch for pinched fingers in folding doors and the hinge side of doors. A door stop helps.

Furniture, TVs and windows

  • Anchor dressers, bookcases and TVs to the wall. A climbing toddler can tip them over. Anchor kits are cheap, and this is one of the most important things on this page.
  • Window cords: go cordless if you can. If not, wrap cords up high on a cleat, well out of reach, and keep the crib away from windows.
  • A screen won’t stop a fall. Use window guards or stops so windows open only a few inches, and don’t put chairs or beds under windows.
  • Soft corner guards on sharp table edges help once they start pulling up.

Outlets, cords and small things

  • Outlet covers on unused outlets, the kind too big to be a choking risk, or sliding covers.
  • Tuck cords behind furniture, out of reach.
  • Button batteries hide in remotes, key fobs, car keys, scales, flameless candles and musical cards. Tape the battery doors shut and keep spares locked away. A swallowed one means the ER right away.
  • Sweep the floor for small things like coins, beads, pins and pills, especially if you have an older kid with small toys. See when the baby is choking.
  • Plastic bags go away, not in a toy box. Tie a knot in dry-cleaning bags before you throw them out.

The bathroom and water

  • Never leave a baby or toddler alone in the bath, not even for a second to grab a towel. A baby can drown in an inch or two of water, quickly and quietly. Take them with you.
  • Empty buckets, coolers and kiddie pools right after you use them.
  • Set your water heater to 120°F or lower, and check the bath water with your wrist or elbow before the baby goes in.
  • Toilet locks and a closed bathroom door keep curious toddlers out.
  • A pool needs a fence on all four sides with a gate that closes and latches by itself.

Heat, fire and smoke

  • Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms on every floor and outside the bedrooms. Test them once a month.
  • Screens or gates around fireplaces, wood stoves and space heaters. Glass on a gas fireplace gets hot enough to burn.
  • Matches and lighters up high and out of sight.

If you keep firearms at home, storage matters more than ever once a baby is crawling. See firearms and a new baby in the house.

You don’t have to do it all this weekend

A list like this can feel like a lot. Do the big ones first: anchor the furniture, lock up medicine and cleaners, gate the stairs, and never leave the baby alone in water. The rest can come a room at a time.

No house is perfectly safe, and no parent catches everything. Baby-proofing is just about lowering the odds, and watching your baby is still the best safety device there is. If something does happen, see when the baby falls off the bed and when the baby is choking.

If it gets dark

If you’re running on empty and things feel too heavy, call or text 988, or the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262. Both are free, 24/7, and dads can call. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

When should I start baby-proofing?

Before the baby starts crawling, usually somewhere around 6 months. Babies move sooner than you expect, so it's easier to have the big things done early. Check again every few months as they reach higher.

What are the most important baby-proofing steps?

Anchor dressers, bookcases and TVs to the wall, lock up medicine and cleaners, put gates at the top and bottom of stairs, keep button batteries out of reach, and never leave a baby alone in the bath or near water.

How do I baby-proof the kitchen?

Lock or empty the cabinets under the sink, cook on the back burners with pot handles turned in, use stove knob covers if your knobs are on the front, keep hot drinks and appliance cords away from the edge, and keep the trash out of reach.

What is the Poison Help number?

1-800-222-1222. It is free, open 24/7, and the experts there will tell you what to do and whether you need the ER. Save it in your phone.
