Feeding

How dads can help with feeding, breast or bottle

Feeding a newborn is a round-the-clock job, and it can feel like there's nothing for you to do if she's breastfeeding. There's plenty. However your family feeds the baby, here's where a dad makes the biggest difference.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Be the setup crew. Water, a snack, phone charger and burp cloth within her reach.
  • Take everything around the feed: the diaper change, the burping, and settling the baby back down.
  • Back her choice. Breast, bottle or both, a fed baby and a rested mom come first.

If she's breastfeeding

  • Bring the baby to her at night, and take the baby back after.
  • Keep her fed and watered. Breastfeeding makes many moms very thirsty and hungry.
  • Handle the burping, changing and rocking so her only job is the feed.
  • Find help early if it hurts or isn't working. A lactation consultant (look for IBCLC) can fix a lot in one visit. Most health plans cover breastfeeding support, so call and ask. If you qualify, WIC offers free breastfeeding help too.
  • Stand between her and the critics. Visitors with opinions can wait outside the conversation.

If she's pumping

  • Wash and dry the pump parts after each use. It's a small job that saves her a lot.
  • Do a night feed with pumped milk so she gets a longer stretch of sleep. Ask her first, since some moms still need to pump around that time.
  • Store milk safely. Label it with the date and follow the storage times her doctor or the CDC gives.

If you're using formula

  • Follow the label exactly. Never add extra water to stretch formula. It can make a baby very sick.
  • Don't heat bottles in the microwave. It makes hot spots that can burn. Warm the bottle in a bowl of warm water and test a drop on your wrist.
  • Toss leftovers. Use a prepared bottle within an hour of starting the feed, and throw out what's left.
  • Set up for the night before bed: clean bottles, formula measured out, water ready. Keep the powder and water separate until it’s time to feed, or keep mixed bottles in the fridge. Mixed formula can only sit out for 2 hours.

Bottle feeding tips for any milk

Hold the baby fairly upright and the bottle flat enough that milk fills the nipple without pouring. This is called paced bottle feeding. Let the baby take breaks. Never prop a bottle and walk away, because a baby can choke. Feeding is great bonding time for dads, so put the phone down and enjoy it. There's more in not feeling bonded yet.

When to call the doctor

  • The baby has fewer wet diapers than the doctor said to expect, or seems very sleepy and hard to wake for feeds
  • The baby isn't gaining weight, or you're worried they're not getting enough
  • Any fever in a baby under 3 months
  • She has a hot, red, painful area on her breast with fever or flu-like aches. That can be an infection, so she should call her own doctor.

When feeding gets emotional

Feeding can bring up a lot: guilt if breastfeeding didn't work out, pressure from others, or feeling like a failure. Remind her that how a baby is fed doesn't decide what kind of mom she is. If she seems deeply sad or anxious, read helping your partner.

Common questions

How can a dad help with breastfeeding?

Bring the baby to her at night, keep her fed and watered, handle burping, changing and settling, find a lactation consultant early if it hurts or isn't working, and keep critics away.

Can dads do night feeds if she is breastfeeding?

Yes, with pumped milk, so she gets a longer stretch of sleep. Ask her first, since some moms still need to pump around that time.

What are the formula safety basics?

Follow the label exactly and never add extra water. Don't heat bottles in the microwave. Use a prepared bottle within an hour of starting the feed and throw out what's left. Never prop a bottle.

When should we call the doctor about feeding?

Call if the baby has fewer wet diapers than expected, is hard to wake for feeds, isn't gaining weight, or has any fever under 3 months. If she has a hot, red, painful area on her breast with fever, she should call her own doctor.
