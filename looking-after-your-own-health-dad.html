Your health

Looking after your own health

When a baby comes, your own health quietly drops to the bottom of the list. Meals become whatever's in reach. Exercise stops. The doctor's appointment you meant to book never happens. It feels selfless, but your family needs you healthy for the long haul. None of this needs to be big. Small and steady is enough.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Drink a full glass of water before bed, and keep one by your side for night feeds.
  • Set out tomorrow's breakfast now, so you eat something real.
  • Put a checkup on your calendar, or a reminder to call and book one.

Eat like it matters

Running on coffee and leftovers wears you down fast. Keep easy food in reach: fruit, nuts, yogurt, eggs, sandwich makings. Eat breakfast, even something small. When people offer help, ask for meals. If cooking is out of the question, delivery counts.

Move a little every day

You probably don't have an hour for the gym. That's fine. A 10-minute walk with the stroller counts. So do a few pushups while the bottle warms, or stretching on the floor during tummy time. Health guidelines for adults suggest about 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, and short bits add up.

Protect what sleep you can

Most adults need at least 7 hours, and new dads rarely get it. Trade off nights, giving each parent one longer stretch. Go to bed early when you can, and nap whenever a window opens. If you lie awake while the baby sleeps, read can't sleep even when the baby sleeps.

Book the checkup

Men are known for skipping the doctor, and new dads even more so. A yearly visit can catch things like high blood pressure, which often has no symptoms. Don't skip the dentist forever either.

Keep your own prescriptions filled. Many insurance plans offer mail-order, often with a 90-day supply, so medicine shows up at your door and you skip the pharmacy line. Ask your insurance or pharmacy what they offer.

Your doctor can help with mood too. How to talk to your doctor as a dad has words you can use.

Notice what you're using to cope

A few extra beers, a lot more caffeine, energy drinks to stay upright, or hours on your phone at 2am. It happens to plenty of dads. If any of it has crept up, read drinking more since the baby.

Don't wait on these

Call 911 for chest pain or pressure, trouble breathing, sudden weakness on one side, or sudden trouble speaking. Don't push through, and don't drive yourself.

Common questions

How can a new dad stay healthy with no time?

Keep it small: easy food in reach, a 10-minute walk with the stroller, a full glass of water, and turns on nights so you get one longer stretch of sleep.

How much exercise does a busy dad need?

Health guidelines for adults suggest about 150 minutes of moderate activity a week. Short bits, like 10-minute walks, add up.

Should new dads get a checkup?

Yes. A yearly visit can catch things like high blood pressure, which often has no symptoms. Your doctor can help with mood and sleep too.

When should I get urgent help for my own health?

Call 911 for chest pain or pressure, trouble breathing, sudden weakness on one side, or sudden trouble speaking. Don't drive yourself.
