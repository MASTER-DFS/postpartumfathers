Family and in-laws

Family visiting after the baby: finding your footing

When a baby arrives, family often shows up to help. That can be a real gift. It can also leave a dad feeling like a guest in his own home, unsure where he fits while everyone else seems to know what to do. Here's how to find your footing.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Claim one job. Bath time, the first morning feed, or bedtime. Keep doing it when family is around.
  • Talk with your partner first about what help you both want and how long visits should be.
  • Take a short break when the house is full. A walk or a drive can reset you.

Feeling sidelined is common

With grandparents holding the baby and giving advice, it's easy to step back and let them take over. Many dads do, then feel left out or less sure of themselves. Stepping back now can make it harder to step in later. You belong in the middle of this too.

Claim your jobs

  • Pick something that's yours, like bath time, the first morning feed, or the bedtime routine. Keep doing it even when family is around.
  • Do skin-to-skin and hold the baby while help handles the dishes and laundry.
  • Learn by doing. If a grandparent has a good trick, try it yourself rather than handing the baby over.

Work as a team on visits

  • Talk with your partner first about what kind of help you both want, how long visits should be, and when you need time alone.
  • Each of you talks to your own family. It usually goes better that way.
  • Point help where it helps most: meals, laundry, the older kids, or a night shift so you can both sleep.
  • Keep it kind. "We're so glad you're here. We'd love help with dinner so we can rest" lands better than a rule.

When advice piles up

Family advice usually comes from love, even when it's outdated. A simple "Thanks, we'll think about it" or "Our doctor suggested this" can keep the peace. For safety things like sleep, follow your baby's doctor.

Look after yourself

  • Take a short break when the house is full. A walk or a drive can reset you.
  • It's okay to miss your quiet home. That's normal, and it doesn't make you ungrateful.
  • If family can't come, that's hard in a different way. Read lonely as a new dad.

If you're feeling on edge or worn down, read overstimulated as a new dad or worn down as a new dad. Other dads get it too: PSI's free dads' groups meet online.

Common questions

How do I deal with in-laws staying after the baby is born?

Talk with your partner first about the help you both want and how long visits should be. Each of you talks to your own family. Point help toward meals, laundry and night shifts.

Why do I feel left out when family visits the new baby?

It's common for dads to feel sidelined when grandparents take over. Claim jobs that are yours, like bath time or a feed, and keep doing them while family is around.

How do I handle unwanted baby advice from family?

Advice usually comes from love. A simple thanks, or saying your doctor suggested something, keeps the peace. For safety things like sleep, follow your baby's doctor.
