Back to work

Going back to work after the baby

For a lot of dads, going back to work comes fast. One week you're home with a newborn, the next you're in a meeting on three hours of sleep. It's a big change, and it's normal for it to feel hard. A few small things make it easier.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

What dads often feel

  • Torn in two. Missing the baby at work, worrying about work at home.
  • Running on empty. Night wakings don't stop because the alarm goes off.
  • Guilty about leaving, or guilty about feeling relieved to have a break. Both are normal.
  • Like nobody at work gets it. Coworkers ask about the baby, rarely about you.

Making it easier

  • Ask about leave and flexibility. Talk to HR about the parental leave, flexible hours or remote days your job offers. Some states have paid family leave, and some workers can take unpaid, job-protected leave under the federal Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). HR can tell you what applies to you.
  • Look for an Employee Assistance Program. Many employers offer one. It usually includes a few free, confidential counseling sessions, and your boss isn't told. Check your benefits site or ask HR.
  • Plan the nights. On work nights, agree at home who does which wake-ups so everyone gets one real stretch of sleep.
  • Keep one moment that's yours with the baby. A morning cuddle before you leave or bath time when you get home.
  • Tell one person at work. A coworker who's a parent can be a lifeline on the hard days.
  • Working from home? Read working from home with a newborn.
  • Use the commute. A few minutes of quiet, music, or a podcast before you walk in the door helps you switch over.

When it's more than adjusting

If you're working late to avoid going home, dreading both work and home, or feeling numb, angry or hopeless for more than two weeks, that can be depression, and it's common in new dads. Read the signs and how to talk to your doctor. You can also talk to other fathers for free at PSI's dads' groups, which meet online.

Common questions

Is it normal for dads to struggle going back to work after a baby?

Yes. Many fathers feel torn, exhausted and guilty when they return to work. It usually eases as routines settle.

Can I get free counseling through my job?

Many employers offer an Employee Assistance Program with a few free, confidential counseling sessions. Check your benefits or ask HR.

Do dads get paternity leave?

It depends on your employer and your state. Some states have paid family leave, and some workers qualify for unpaid, job-protected leave under the federal FMLA. Ask HR what applies to you.
