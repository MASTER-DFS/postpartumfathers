Dads doing it on their own

Raising your baby on your own? You’ve got this

Dads end up raising a baby on their own, or most of the time, for all kinds of reasons: a separation, custody, a partner who is ill or away, adoption, or a loss. However you got here, it can mean every night feed, every diaper and every decision is yours. That's a lot for anyone. If you're tired and overwhelmed, that's not failure. It's what happens when one person does the work of two. You can do this, and you don't have to do it without help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Build your team

  • Make a list of who can help, even a little: family, friends, neighbors, coworkers.
  • Ask for specific things. "Can you watch her Saturday from 2 to 4 so I can sleep?" works better than "let me know if you can help."
  • Have a backup for when you're sick or work runs late.
  • Talk to your baby's doctor. They often know local programs for parents raising a baby alone.

Make it doable

  • Sleep when you can, even 20 minutes. Rest beats chores.
  • Keep it simple. Easy meals, a basic routine, a house that's lived in.
  • Set up stations with diapers, wipes and a change of clothes in the rooms you use most.
  • Check your work benefits for leave, flexible hours, or dependent care accounts.
  • Dial 211 for free, local help with food, childcare and bills.

Looking after you

You're the one your baby counts on, so taking care of yourself is part of the job. Fatherhood.gov has free resources for dads, and other fathers are on our message board and at PSI's free dads' groups. If you're feeling worn down, numb or hopeless, read worn down as a new dad and how to talk to your doctor.

Common questions

How do dads cope raising a baby on their own?

Build a support team, ask for specific help, keep things simple, sleep when you can, and use local programs and workplace benefits.

Where can dads raising a baby alone get help?

Dial 211 for free local help, ask your baby's doctor about programs for single parents, and see Fatherhood.gov for resources for dads.

Is it normal to feel overwhelmed raising a baby alone?

Yes. Doing it alone is a lot. If overwhelm turns into numbness or hopelessness for more than two weeks, talk to a doctor.
