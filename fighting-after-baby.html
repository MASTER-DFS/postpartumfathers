You’re not broken, you’re exhausted

Shutting down or snapping: arguing after a baby

When you’re running on broken sleep and nonstop stress, it doesn’t take much to start a fight. A lot of dads fall into the same trap: either you go completely silent and don’t say a word because you’re trying not to blow up, or you snap and say something you regret two seconds later. Neither one makes you a bad partner. It means your fuse is gone, and you need a safer way to hit pause.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Why it happens

  • Going silent is an emergency brake. Most guys don’t go quiet to be cold or to punish their partner. You shut down because your brain is flooded and you know that if you open your mouth, something mean is coming out.
  • To an exhausted partner, silence feels like being left alone with it. When you go quiet without explaining why, she may push harder to get a reaction, which backs you further into a corner until you finally snap.
  • Sleep deprivation wrecks your filter. Everything feels bigger and more personal at 3am.
  • In dads, depression often looks like anger. A short fuse, irritability and feeling trapped are some of the most common signs of postpartum depression in dads.

What to do instead of shutting down or snapping

  • Announce the pause before you go quiet. You don’t have to talk when you’re angry, and nothing gets solved in the middle of the night anyway. Just say why you’re going quiet, and that you’re coming back.
  • Put a time on it, and actually come back. Take 20 minutes. Step outside, drink some water, or wash bottles in silence until your heart rate drops. Even if you come back only to say “I’m still wiped out, let’s talk tomorrow after we sleep”, you kept your word.
  • Agree on a 3am amnesty rule. Decide during the day that nothing said between 11pm and 6am counts as a real relationship conversation. At night, stick to two-word logistics: your turn, need water? Everything else waits for daylight.
  • Make the apology fast and clean. When you do say something you regret, don’t let shame keep you hiding for two days, and don’t add “but you…” on the end. Say it plainly: “That came out mean. I’m exhausted and I took it out on you. I’m sorry.”

Words to use when you’re on edge

Sometimes it’s easier to send a text from the other room than to say it out loud in the middle of it. Copy one of these and change anything that doesn’t sound like you.

“I'm not ignoring you. I'm really on edge right now and I don't want to say something I'll regret. Give me 20 minutes to cool off and we'll talk.”

“I'm not mad at you. I've been on edge and running on empty lately, and I think I need to talk to someone so I can show up better for us.”

“Hey, we're in the thick of it with the baby and running on no sleep. Any chance you could drop off food or hold the baby for an hour this weekend so we can sleep?”

There are more of these, for your boss, your doctor and your family, on what to say when you can’t find the words.

If you’re afraid you might lose control

If you feel like you might slam something, yell at the baby, or lose your temper with your partner, put the baby safely on their back in their crib, walk into another room or step outside, and call or text 988 right now. Asking for backup before something happens is the strong thing to do. Never shake a baby, not even for a second: if you think a baby may have been shaken, call 911 straight away.

Protect your sleep: a 2-day night-shift plan

Planning a whole week with a newborn isn’t realistic, because everything changes too fast. Just plan the next 48 hours. Getting each of you one uninterrupted four-to-five hour block is the fastest way to stop the fighting.

Early shift8pm to 1am, both nights

You are on. Your partner is asleep, and that is a five hour block she does not get woken for.

Late shift1am to 6am, both nights

Your partner is on. You are asleep, and that is your five hour block.

Morning reset6am to 8am, both days

Coffee, a feed, and a 20 minute shower each. Nobody starts the day without one.

Daytime tap-out20 to 30 minutes, both days

One solo break each. A walk, the gym, or a nap. No baby and no chores in it.

  • Off duty means off duty. Whoever is sleeping goes in another room, or uses earplugs or white noise, and is woken only for an emergency.
  • The tap-out rule. Any time either of you feels your temper spiking, say “I need 10”. Put the baby safely in the crib or bassinet, and swap or step outside. No questions, no scorekeeping.
  • If she is breastfeeding. During her 8pm to 1am block you can give a bottle, or bring the baby in strictly to nurse and take the baby straight back out to burp, change and settle.
  • Swap the halves if one is clearly worse. Whoever gets wrecked two nights running takes the other shift next time.

Common questions

Why do I go completely silent during arguments with my partner?

Many men go silent when they are overwhelmed because their brain is flooded with stress and they are trying not to say something angry that they will regret. Tell your partner out loud that you aren't ignoring her, you just need 20 minutes to cool down so you can talk calmly.

Why are my partner and I fighting so much after having a baby?

Severe sleep deprivation, round-the-clock stress, and having no time to recover wear down your patience. Frequent anger and irritability can also be signs of postpartum depression or anxiety in either parent.

How do we stop keeping score with a newborn?

Stop planning a whole week and focus on the next 48 hours. Split the night into two shifts so each parent gets at least one four-to-five-hour block of uninterrupted sleep, and agree on a no-questions-asked tap-out phrase when either of you hits your limit.
