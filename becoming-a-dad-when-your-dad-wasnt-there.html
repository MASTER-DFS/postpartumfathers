Breaking the cycle

Becoming a dad when your own dad wasn't there

Maybe your dad left. Maybe he was in the house but checked out, or angry, or hard to be around. Now you're a dad yourself, and you may be wondering how to do something nobody showed you. Plenty of great fathers started exactly here. Not having a model doesn't mean you can't be one.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Write down one thing you wished your dad had done. Then do it this week.
  • Think of one man who showed you how, such as a coach, an uncle or a friend's dad.
  • Say it out loud to someone you trust: "I want to do this differently."

Why it's coming up now

Fatherhood tends to drag your own childhood to the surface. Holding your baby can stir up anger, sadness or questions you thought were settled. You might feel it at odd times, like during bath time or when your kid reaches for you. That's normal. It's your mind making sense of where you came from while you figure out where you're going.

You're not doomed to repeat it

Many men fear they'll turn into their father. Worrying about it is a good sign. It means you're paying attention. Patterns can be broken, and they usually break because one person decides to do it differently, then keeps choosing to, day after day.

Learn it on purpose

Nobody is born knowing how to be a dad. Most men learn by watching. If you didn't have that, you can learn another way:

  • Borrow from good men you know. Notice how they talk to their kids, and ask them questions.
  • Take a class. Many hospitals and community centers run classes for dads.
  • Read and listen. Fatherhood.gov offers free advice and lists of fatherhood programs.
  • Just show up. Being around, every day, is most of the job.

When old anger shows up

Sometimes the thing you swore you'd never do comes out of your mouth anyway. A yell, a tone, a slammed door. When it happens, stop, cool off and repair it. Tell your child you're sorry, even if they're small. Kids learn a lot from a dad who owns his mistakes. That may be the very thing you never got.

If your temper scares you, read overstimulated as a new dad, and talk to someone.

Getting support

Talking with a counselor can untangle what you brought from childhood, so it doesn't steer you as a parent. Finding a therapist as a dad walks you through it. If your dad is trying to get back in touch now, see when a parent you're distant from reaches out.

  • Our message board, open any time.
  • PSI's free dads' groups, which meet online.

Common questions

Can I be a good dad if my father wasn't there?

Yes. Many great fathers grew up without a good model. Worrying about it means you're paying attention, and patterns break when one person keeps choosing to do it differently.

How do I learn to be a dad with no role model?

Borrow from good men you know, take a class for dads, use free tips from Fatherhood.gov, and keep showing up. Being there every day is most of the job.

What if I act like my dad did?

Stop, cool off and repair it. Tell your child you're sorry. A dad who owns his mistakes teaches a lot. If your temper scares you, talk to someone.

Why is my childhood coming up now that I'm a dad?

Becoming a father often stirs up old feelings about your own childhood. It's normal, and talking with a counselor can help you sort through it.
