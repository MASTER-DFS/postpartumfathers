When the baby gets hurt

When the baby slips down in a high chair

You’re out to eat. The high chair has no straps, or one that’s broken. You look at the menu, and the baby slides down through the front of the seat and gets stuck. Your heart stops. This happens to careful parents more than you’d think. Here’s what to check before you sit them down, what to do if it happens, and when to get them looked at.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Right now

  • Support their body and lift it up. Don’t pull on the head.
  • Call 911 if they can’t breathe, turn blue or gray, or go limp.
  • Out and seems fine? Watch them closely for the rest of the day, and call the pediatrician if anything is off.

If the baby is stuck right now

  1. Get under them and take their weight. Put your hands under their bottom and chest and lift the body up, so the seat isn’t pressing on their neck or chest.
  2. Don’t pull the head up or out. That can make it worse.
  3. Once the weight is off, ease them back up and out the way they went in.
  4. If they still won’t come free, keep holding their weight and shout for help. Staff can tip the chair back, take off the tray, or pull the chair away from the table while you hold the baby.
  5. If they can’t breathe, turn blue or gray, or go limp, call 911 the moment their body is supported. Someone else can make the call while you hold them.

Stay as calm as you can. Your voice helps them stay calm too.

Call 911 or go to the ER after they’re free if the baby:

  • Has trouble breathing, or breathing that sounds noisy or squeaky
  • Has a hoarse or strange cry, or a voice that sounds different
  • Has trouble swallowing, or is drooling more than usual
  • Throws up
  • Is very sleepy, floppy or hard to wake, or passed out even for a moment
  • Has red marks, bruising or swelling on the neck
  • Has tiny red dots on the face or in the whites of the eyes
  • Just doesn’t seem like themselves, or anything looks wrong to you

If they seem fine

A baby who cries, calms down, and goes back to eating, looking around and acting like themselves is usually okay. Some redness where the seat pressed is common and should fade.

  • Watch them closely for the rest of the day. Some signs show up later. Look for anything on the list above.
  • Call the pediatrician the same day if anything pressed on the neck, even if they seem fine. Most offices have a nurse line or an on-call doctor after hours.

You are not overreacting by calling. It’s a short call that puts your mind at rest or catches something early.

Before you sit them down

Restaurant high chairs take a beating. Straps go missing, buckles break, trays wobble. A quick check takes ten seconds.

  • Look for straps. A good high chair has a strap or post between the legs and a belt around the waist. That piece between the legs is what stops a baby sliding down and out.
  • Make sure they work. Click the buckle. Tug the strap. If it’s missing, broken or loose, ask for another chair.
  • The tray is not a seat belt. It’s for food. It won’t hold a baby in.
  • Check for wobble. Push on the chair. If it rocks or a leg is loose, swap it.
  • Ask what else they have. Some places have newer chairs in the back, or a different style. It’s fine to ask.
  • Bring your own backup. A portable high chair seat or fabric seat harness that straps to a regular chair packs small and goes anywhere. If you use a seat that clips onto the table, make sure it locks on, the table is sturdy enough, and the baby’s feet can’t reach a table leg to push off.
  • Hold them on your lap if nothing there is safe. It’s not ideal for eating, but it’s safer than a chair with no straps.

Newer high chairs sold in the US, including restaurant ones, must have a strap or post between the legs and a three-point belt. Plenty of restaurants still use older chairs, so check every time.

While they’re in the chair

  • Sit close. Put the high chair next to you, not across the table, so you can reach them fast.
  • Keep a hand nearby when they start to wiggle, stand up or slump down.
  • Never let them stand in the chair, and keep older kids from climbing on it.
  • Keep the chair away from the table edge if they can push off it with their feet. A hard push can tip a chair back.
  • Move hot drinks, plates, knives and placemats out of reach.
  • Never leave them alone in the chair, even to grab a napkin.

The same goes for high chairs at home. Use the straps every time, including the one between the legs. There’s more in baby-proofing your home.

The shaky feeling after

A scare like this can stay with you. You might replay it all night. You might not want to eat out for a while. You might be angry at the restaurant, or at yourself. All of that is normal.

You noticed, you got them out, and you checked them over. That’s exactly what a good dad does. Next time you’ll check the chair first.

If the worry won’t switch off, and you keep checking on the baby or can’t settle long after they’re fine, that counts too. See anxious and on edge.

If it gets dark

If you’re running on empty and things feel too heavy, call or text 988, or the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262. Both are free, 24/7, and dads can call. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

My baby slid down in the high chair and got stuck. What do I do?

Get your hands under their bottom and chest and lift the body up to take the weight off their neck and chest. Don't pull on the head. Ease them back out the way they went in. If they won't come free, keep holding them and shout for help. Call 911 if they can't breathe, turn blue or gray, or go limp.

When should I take my baby to the ER after getting stuck in a high chair?

Go right away if they have trouble breathing, a hoarse or strange cry, trouble swallowing or drooling, throw up, are very sleepy or hard to wake, have marks or swelling on the neck, tiny red dots on the face or eyes, or just don't seem like themselves.

The restaurant high chair has no straps. What should I do?

Ask for another chair that has working straps, including the one between the legs. If they don't have one, use your own portable seat or harness, or hold the baby on your lap.

Is the high chair tray enough to keep my baby in?

No. The tray is for food. Use the straps every time, including the strap or post between the legs.
