Worried about a new dad? How to help

This page is for moms, partners and family. If a new dad in your life doesn't seem like himself, you're right to notice. About 1 in 10 dads go through postpartum depression, according to Postpartum Support International, and many never hear that it can happen to fathers. Here's what to look for and how to help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Signs a new dad may be struggling

In men, depression and anxiety often don't look like sadness. You might notice:

  • Irritability, a short fuse, or anger
  • Pulling away from the family or friends
  • Working longer hours, or staying away from home
  • Drinking more, gaming, or endless scrolling
  • Seeming numb, flat, or hopeless
  • Trouble sleeping even when the baby sleeps
  • Constant worry, or headaches and stomach problems

A rough week is normal with a newborn. If several of these last more than two weeks, it's worth bringing up.

How to bring it up

  • Pick a calm moment, not in the middle of a hard night.
  • Start with what you see, gently. "You've seemed really worn down lately. How are you doing?"
  • Listen more than you talk. He may not have words for it yet.
  • Let him know it's common and treatable, and that it doesn't make him a bad dad.
  • Offer one small next step, like sharing this site, or helping him book a doctor's appointment.

Places he can start

  • Our guides for dads. Postpartum depression in dads and worn down as a new dad are good first reads. Everything is on the guides page.
  • Other dads. PSI's free dads' groups meet online, and our community board is open any time.
  • A doctor. Read how to talk to your doctor.
  • Postpartum Support International's help for dads page.

If you're worried about his safety

If he talks about being better off gone, or about harming himself, call or text 988. You can call for someone you're worried about. In an emergency, call 911.

Look after yourself too

Caring for a newborn and worrying about someone you love is a lot. The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, is free, 24/7, and takes calls and texts from moms, dads and family.

Common questions

How do I know if my husband has postpartum depression?

In dads it often looks like irritability, anger, pulling away, working longer hours, drinking more, or seeming numb. If several signs last more than two weeks, gently bring it up and suggest a doctor.

Can fathers get postpartum depression?

Yes. About 1 in 10 dads experience postpartum depression, according to Postpartum Support International. It's treatable.

How do I talk to a new dad about his mental health?

Pick a calm moment, say what you've noticed gently, listen, and offer one small next step like sharing a resource or booking a doctor's visit.

What if I'm worried about a new dad's safety?

If he talks about being better off gone or harming himself, call or text 988. You can call for someone you're worried about. In an emergency, call 911.
