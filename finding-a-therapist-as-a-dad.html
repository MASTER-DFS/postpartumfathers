Getting help

Finding a therapist as a dad, and paying for it

Deciding to talk to someone is a big step, and a strong one. The next questions are usually: how do I find the right person, and what's this going to cost? Here's a simple way to start, and some ways to make it affordable.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Find a therapist in the PSI directory

Free to search. Lists therapists in the US and Canada who specialize in new parents, including dads.

Where to find one

  • The PSI directory lists therapists who specialize in new parents, including dads.
  • Your insurance. Call the number on your card or check their website for in-network therapists.
  • Your doctor can give you a referral.
  • FindTreatment.gov is the free government finder for mental health care near you.
  • Video therapy means you can do it from your car or a quiet room, no commute needed.

Keeping the cost down

  • Employee Assistance Program. Many employers offer a few free, confidential counseling sessions. Check your benefits or ask HR.
  • Use your insurance. Seeing an in-network therapist usually costs much less. Ask what your copay will be.
  • Ask about a sliding scale. Many therapists lower their fee based on income. It's fine to ask.
  • Community mental health centers often charge based on what you can pay.
  • Training clinics at universities may offer lower-cost sessions with supervised counselors.
  • If you own firearms and would rather not have that become the topic, Walk the Talk America keeps a directory of providers who understand gun ownership. More in firearms and a new baby.
  • Free support groups like PSI's free dads' groups can help while you look, or alongside therapy.

What to expect

The first session is mostly talking: what's going on, how long, and what you'd like to change. You don't need to have the right words. It's fine to try a few people until one feels right. A good fit matters more than anything else.

Common questions

How do dads find a therapist who understands new parents?

Search the PSI directory, check your insurance's in-network list, ask your doctor for a referral, or use FindTreatment.gov.

How can I afford therapy?

Check for free sessions through an Employee Assistance Program, use in-network insurance, ask about a sliding scale, or try community mental health centers and university training clinics.

What happens at the first therapy session?

Mostly talking about what's going on and what you want to change. It's fine to try a few therapists until one feels right.
