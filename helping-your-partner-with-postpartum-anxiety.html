Supporting your partner

Helping your wife or partner with postpartum anxiety

Every new parent worries. But if she can't switch it off, checks the baby over and over, or lies awake braced for something to go wrong, it may be postpartum anxiety. It's common, it's not her fault, and it's very treatable. Here's how to help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Take one worry job off her. Say, "I'll check on the baby tonight. You rest."
  • Listen first. Ask what the worry feels like before you try to fix it.
  • Help her get care. The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, is free and 24/7, and takes calls and texts from partners too.

What postpartum anxiety can look like

Postpartum anxiety is common. Postpartum Support International says about 1 in 5 new moms and dads have depression or anxiety symptoms in the first year. Anxiety can show up with or without sadness. You might notice:

  • Constant worry she can't turn off, often about the baby's health or safety
  • Checking the baby's breathing again and again
  • Trouble sleeping, even when the baby sleeps
  • Racing thoughts, restlessness, or not being able to sit still
  • Irritability or anger that isn't like her
  • A racing heart, tight chest, shaky hands or upset stomach
  • Panic attacks: sudden waves of fear that feel physical
  • Avoiding things, like leaving the house or letting anyone else hold the baby

Some moms also get scary, unwanted thoughts or images of the baby getting hurt. They hate these thoughts, and having them doesn't mean she'll act on them. Sometimes this is called postpartum OCD. It's treatable too, and PSI runs free online support groups for it, along with groups for anxiety and mood.

What helps

  • Take the worry seriously. "That sounds exhausting. I'm here."
  • Don't argue with the fear. Telling her it's silly doesn't make it stop, and it can make her stop telling you.
  • Go easy on endless reassurance. Answering the same worry over and over can keep the loop going. Be kind, then gently change the subject or do something together.
  • Protect her sleep. A tired brain worries more. Take a night feed or give her a solid block of hours.
  • Slow things down together. A short walk outside, or a few slow breaths side by side, can take the edge off.
  • Cut down on late-night searching. Offer to look things up for her, or save questions for the doctor.

Getting her care

If the worry lasts more than two weeks, or gets in the way of sleeping, eating or daily life, it's time to talk to a professional. Her OB, midwife or family doctor is a good first call. Talk therapy works well for anxiety, and some moms also take medication that's safe while breastfeeding.

The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, is free, 24/7, by call or text, and can help her or you figure out next steps. The PSI HelpLine, 1-800-944-4773, connects families with local providers. It isn't a crisis line, and a volunteer replies 8am to 11pm ET.

If she isn't ready to get help, read what to do when she won't get help.

When to get help right away

  • A panic attack can feel like a heart attack. If she has chest pain or trouble breathing and you're not sure, call 911. Say she recently gave birth.
  • If she talks about not wanting to be here, or about harming herself or the baby, call or text 988 together, or 911 if anyone is in danger right now.

Look after yourself too

Living with someone else's worry is tiring, and anxiety can rub off. Keep up your own sleep and food as best you can, and talk to someone. If you're anxious yourself, read anxious and on edge. PSI's free dads' groups meet online, and our message board is open any time.

Postpartum anxiety gets better with support and treatment. Most moms feel like themselves again.

Common questions

What are the signs of postpartum anxiety?

Constant worry she can't turn off, checking the baby over and over, trouble sleeping even when the baby sleeps, racing thoughts, a racing heart or upset stomach, panic attacks, and avoiding things like leaving the house.

How can I help my wife with postpartum anxiety?

Take the worry seriously without arguing with it, go easy on repeating the same reassurance, protect her sleep, and help her talk to her doctor. The Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262 takes calls and texts from partners, 24/7.

Is postpartum anxiety treatable?

Yes. Talk therapy works well, and some moms also take medication that is safe while breastfeeding. Most moms feel like themselves again with support and treatment.

When is it an emergency?

If she has chest pain or trouble breathing, call 911 and say she recently gave birth. If she talks about not wanting to be here or harming herself or the baby, call or text 988 together, or 911 if anyone is in danger right now.
