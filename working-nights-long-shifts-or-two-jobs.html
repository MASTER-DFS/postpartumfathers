Work

Working nights, long shifts or two jobs

When you work nights, long shifts or two jobs, a new baby can leave you running on fumes and missing them at the same time. You can’t change the job tonight. But you can protect your sleep, stay safe on the road, and make the time you do get count.

If you’re thinking about suicide, please call or text 988 any time. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Pick your anchor sleep block and tell your partner when it is.
  • If you’re nodding off on the drive home, pull over and nap. Every time.
  • Choose one daily thing with the baby that’s yours, like bath time or the first bottle.

When you’re never really off

Nurses, truckers, cops, warehouse and factory crews, restaurant workers, dads holding down two jobs. When you work nights or long hours, a newborn means you can go weeks without real sleep. You’re also missing time with your baby, and that can sting.

Protect your sleep like it’s part of the job

Because it is. Tired is how mistakes and accidents happen, at work and at home.

  • Pick one “anchor” sleep block of four or more hours at the same time every day, even on days off.
  • Make the room dark and quiet. Blackout curtains or foil on the window, a fan for white noise.
  • Agree on who covers the baby during your sleep block. Write it down so nobody has to guess.
  • Earplugs only when someone else is on duty. If you’re the one listening for the baby, you need to hear them.
  • Nap before a night shift if you can, even 20 minutes.

Don’t drive drowsy

The drive home after a long shift is one of the most dangerous parts of the day. If your eyes keep closing, you drift, or you can’t remember the last few miles, pull over somewhere safe and nap. Coffee helps a little, not enough. Never drive the baby when you’re that tired.

Make the most of the time you get

  • Pick one daily thing that’s yours. The first bottle when you get home, bath time, or a song before your shift.
  • Short and every day beats long and rare. Fifteen minutes of real attention counts.
  • Leave a voice note or video for your baby to hear while you’re gone.
  • See not feeling bonded yet and things to do with your baby.

Keep your partner in the loop

When you’re on opposite schedules, you can go days without a real conversation. A shared calendar and a two-minute check-in at handoff help a lot. “How was your day? What do you need from me before I go?” See taking night shifts with a newborn.

Know when it’s too much

Constant exhaustion can look a lot like depression, and it can turn into it. If you’re snapping at everyone, feel numb, or can’t sleep even when you finally get the chance, read is this more than tired? and worn down and running on empty. PSI’s free dads’ groups meet online, and our message board is open any time.

If it gets dark

If you’re thinking about suicide, or that your family would be better off without you, call or text 988. It’s free, 24/7, and you don’t have to be in crisis to call. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

How do I sleep with a newborn when I work nights?

Pick one anchor sleep block of four or more hours at the same time every day, make the room dark and quiet, and agree in writing on who covers the baby while you sleep. Only wear earplugs when someone else is on duty.

Is it dangerous to drive home after a long shift?

Yes, drowsy driving is a real danger. If your eyes keep closing or you drift, pull over somewhere safe and nap. Never drive the baby when you're that tired.

How can I bond with my baby when I work long hours?

Pick one daily thing that's yours, like the first bottle when you get home or bath time. Short and every day beats long and rare.
