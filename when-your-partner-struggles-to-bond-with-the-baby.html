Your partner

When your partner struggles to feel close to the baby

She says she doesn’t feel anything for the baby, or worse. Hearing that is scary. You’re not alone, she’s not a bad mother, and it can get better with help. Here’s what you can do.

If she talks about harming the baby or herself and might act on it, or seems confused or out of touch with reality, call 911. More on getting help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Stay calm and listen. “Thank you for telling me. That sounds really hard.”
  • Take the baby so she can rest. Your care keeps the baby close and safe.
  • Not sure how serious it is? Call 1-833-852-6262, any time.

It happens more than people say

Not every mom feels an instant rush of love. For some it grows over weeks. For others, especially with postpartum depression or anxiety, the feelings seem to go missing. Some say they feel nothing for the baby. Some say harsher things that are scary to hear.

Pulling away from the baby is a known sign of postpartum depression. It doesn’t make her a bad mother, and it doesn’t mean it will stay this way. With help, the bond often grows.

What helps day to day

  • Keep calm when she says it. Arguing or acting shocked can make her shut down. “Thank you for telling me. That sounds really hard.”
  • Don’t push the bond. Small, low-pressure moments help more than “just hold the baby.” A bath together, skin-to-skin while she rests, a walk with the stroller.
  • Protect her sleep. Exhaustion makes everything feel worse. Splitting the nights helps.
  • Step in with the baby. Your love and care keep the baby safe and close while she heals. That’s not taking over. It’s holding the fort.
  • Keep the house running so she isn’t drowning. See helping her recover.

Help her get care

Tell her what you’ve noticed, without blame. Offer to make the call with her or go to the appointment. See helping your partner for the words to use, and when she won’t get help if she says she’s fine.

If she’s already getting treatment and it isn’t helping, that’s worth telling her doctor. There are usually other options to try.

When to get help fast

  • Call 911 if she talks about harming the baby or herself and it seems like she might act on it, seems confused or out of touch with reality, sees or hears things that aren’t there, or hasn’t slept for days. These can be signs of postpartum psychosis, a medical emergency. Keep the baby with you or another adult.
  • Unwanted scary thoughts that horrify her, and that she’d never act on, are common with postpartum anxiety. They still deserve a call to her doctor. See when your partner is anxious all the time.
  • Not sure? Call or text the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262. It’s free, 24/7, and you can call on your own to talk through what you’re seeing.

Look after yourself

Hearing your partner say she doesn’t love the baby hurts, and it can leave you feeling alone, scared and angry all at once. You need support too. Talk to someone you trust, and watch for the signs in yourself. PSI’s free dads’ groups meet online, and our message board is open any time.

If it gets dark

If anyone in the home is thinking about suicide, call or text 988, free and 24/7. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

What if my wife says she doesn't love the baby?

It happens more than people say, and pulling away from the baby is a known sign of postpartum depression. Stay calm, thank her for telling you, take on more of the baby's care, and help her get care. With help, the bond often grows.

Is it postpartum depression if she doesn't feel bonded?

It can be. Only a doctor can say, but not feeling close to the baby is a known sign of postpartum depression and anxiety. Both are common and treatable.

When is it an emergency?

Call 911 if she talks about harming the baby or herself and might act on it, seems confused or out of touch with reality, sees or hears things that aren't there, or hasn't slept for days. If you're not sure, call the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262.
