How you're feeling

Snapping at everyone since the baby?

You bite someone's head off over nothing, then feel awful about it. Lots of new dads find their fuse has gotten very short. It doesn't make you a bad person. It's a sign something needs attention, and it can get better.

If you ever feel close to hurting someone, or yourself, you don't have to wait for it to get worse. Step away, and call or text 988 any time, day or night. It's for any kind of emotional crisis, not only suicide. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911. More crisis help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Leave the room before you say it. "I need five minutes" is enough.
  • If you're holding the baby, lay the baby on their back in the crib first, then step out.
  • Fix it later in one line: "I snapped. That was on me. I'm sorry."

Why it happens

Anger rarely comes out of nowhere. Months of broken sleep, money worries, a job that didn't get easier, and no time to yourself all shorten the fuse. When you're running on empty, small things like a dropped bottle or a question asked twice can feel huge.

There's something else worth knowing. In men, depression often shows up as anger and irritability rather than sadness. Plenty of dads never feel "depressed". They just feel angry, tense, or fed up most of the time. If that sounds familiar, it's worth a closer look in the signs in dads.

In the moment

  • Notice the early signs. Tight jaw, hot face, clenched hands, raised voice. That's your cue to pause.
  • Step away. Another room, the porch, a walk around the block. Say you'll be back, so it isn't a slammed door.
  • Slow your breathing. Breathe out longer than you breathe in, a few times. Splash cold water on your face.
  • Never drive angry, and never handle the baby while you're boiling over. The baby is safe crying in the crib for a few minutes.
  • Come back when you're calmer, not when you've won the argument in your head.

If a crying baby is what sets you off, read when the crying won't stop and when the crying makes you cringe.

After you've snapped

Everyone loses it sometimes. What matters is what you do next. Go back and own it in a sentence, without a "but". With a toddler or older kid, keep it simple: "Daddy got too loud. I'm sorry. I love you." Kids learn a lot from watching a grown-up repair things.

Then look for the pattern. Is it always late at night, after work, or when you haven't eaten? Small changes there often help more than willpower. If you and your partner keep ending up in the same fight, see shutting down or snapping at home.

Lower the pressure day to day

  • Protect one real block of sleep. A plan like taking night shifts can change a lot.
  • Move your body. A short walk or a workout burns off some of the tension.
  • Watch the drinking. Alcohol makes a short fuse shorter. See drinking more since the baby.
  • Get a break that's yours, even twenty minutes, without guilt.
  • Say it to someone. Another dad, a friend, or our message board, under a username.

When to get help

It's time to talk to a doctor or therapist if you're angry most days for more than two weeks, if people at home seem nervous around you, if you've broken things or scared yourself, or if you feel numb or hopeless underneath the anger. Anger is very treatable, and treating depression often takes the edge off the irritability too.

You don't have to say the word depression. "I've been angry all the time since the baby, and it's not like me" is a fine way to start. See how to talk to your doctor and finding a therapist.

Common questions

Is it normal to be angry all the time after having a baby?

It is common. Lack of sleep, stress and no time to yourself all shorten the fuse. If it lasts most days for more than two weeks, talk to a doctor, because anger can be a sign of depression in dads.

Can anger be a sign of depression in men?

Yes. In men, depression often shows up as anger, irritability and pulling away rather than sadness. It is treatable, and treatment often eases the irritability.

What should I do when I feel like I am about to snap?

Step away before you say it. If you are holding the baby, lay the baby on their back in the crib first. Breathe out slowly, and come back when you are calmer.

How do I make it right after I yell?

Go back and own it in one sentence without a "but", such as "I snapped. That was on me. I'm sorry." With young kids, keep it short and warm.
