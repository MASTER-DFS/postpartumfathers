Plan 48 hours, not a whole week

Taking night shifts with a newborn: a 2-day plan

When both of you wake up for every feed and every cry, neither of you ever gets real rest. Planning a whole week with a newborn isn’t realistic, because things change too fast. Just plan the next 48 hours. Getting each of you one uninterrupted four-to-five hour block is the fastest way to feel human again, and to stop shutting down or snapping at each other.

Jump to the 2-night schedule

Try this tonight

  • Split the night into two blocks, so each of you gets four to five hours straight.
  • Off duty means off duty. Another room, door closed, earplugs or a fan.
  • Never fall asleep holding the baby on a bed, a couch or a chair.

Why one long block beats taking turns all night

  • Broken sleep doesn’t add up. Six hours in twenty-minute pieces does not do what four unbroken hours does. If you both wake for everything, you both run on fragments.
  • Somebody is awake anyway. The baby is getting looked after either way. The only question is whether one of you is asleep while it happens.
  • It takes the scorekeeping out of it. When the shifts are agreed in advance, nobody is lying there doing sums about who did more.
  • Exhaustion is doing most of the arguing. Fix the sleep first and a lot of the friction goes with it.

How the split works

  • Two nights at a time. Don’t worry about next week. Just agree on tonight and tomorrow night.
  • Off duty means off duty. Whoever is sleeping goes in another room, closes the door, and uses earplugs or a fan. They get woken only for a real emergency. Lying in the same room “just in case” means neither of you sleeps.
  • One protected block beats eight broken hours. Four to five straight hours does far more for your brain and your mood than eight hours cut into 45 minute pieces.
  • Off duty also means without the baby. Never fall asleep holding the baby on a bed, a couch or a chair. The baby sleeps alone, on their back, in their own crib, every time.
  • The tap-out rule. At any point, day or night, if either of you feels your temper spiking or the crying won’t stop, say “I need 10”. Put the baby safely in the crib or bassinet and swap, or step outside. No questions asked and no scorekeeping.

Making it work if she’s breastfeeding

  • One bottle. She pumps earlier in the day, and you give one bottle of breast milk or formula during the first block, so she sleeps five hours straight.
  • Bring and take back. If she is nursing every feed, you still take the first shift, with the baby in another room. When the baby wakes hungry you bring the baby to her in bed strictly to nurse, then take the baby straight back out to burp, change and settle. She never gets out of bed or turns on a bright light.

The 2-day schedule

Here is the version most families land on. Print it, stick it on the fridge, and do the next two days when you get there.

Early shift8pm to 1am, both nights

You are on. Your partner is asleep, and that is a five hour block she does not get woken for.

Late shift1am to 6am, both nights

Your partner is on. You are asleep, and that is your five hour block.

Morning reset6am to 8am, both days

Coffee, a feed, and a 20 minute shower each. Nobody starts the day without one.

Daytime tap-out20 to 30 minutes, both days

One solo break each. A walk, the gym, or a nap. No baby and no chores in it.

Common questions

How do parents split night shifts with a newborn?

The simplest split is an early shift, such as 8pm to 1am, and a late shift, 1am to 6am. The off-duty parent sleeps in a separate room with earplugs or white noise so both parents get one uninterrupted four-to-five-hour block of sleep.

Why only plan a 2-day schedule with a newborn?

Newborns change constantly, and trying to stick to a seven-day schedule usually falls apart and causes frustration. Planning just the next 48 hours feels doable when you are exhausted, and lets you adjust every two days.

What if I still can't sleep during my off-duty shift?

Many new dads feel wired and exhausted at the same time. Sleeping in a separate room away from the baby monitor helps your brain switch off. There is more in our guide on not being able to sleep even when the baby does.

Can we take night shifts if my partner is breastfeeding?

Yes. She can pump earlier in the day so you give one bottle during her sleep block, or you keep the baby in another room and bring them to her strictly to nurse, then take the baby straight back out to burp, change and settle so she stays in bed.
