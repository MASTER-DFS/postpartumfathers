When your family is far away

A new baby is a time when you want your people close. If your parents and family live in another state or another country, you feel that every day. Here's how to let family help from far away, and how to build some support right where you are.

If you're ever thinking about suicide or feel your family would be better off without you, please call or text 988 any time, day or night. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911. More crisis help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Set up one regular call with family, same day and time each week, so the baby gets to know them.
  • Tell one person nearby that your family is far away. Most people want to help but don't know how.
  • Call 211 tomorrow and ask about free parent groups near you.

Why it hits so hard

For a lot of families, a new baby used to mean grandparents, aunts and uncles close by. Someone to bring food, hold the baby while you sleep, or just tell you you're doing fine. When your family lives in another state or another country, you feel that missing piece every day.

  • There's no backup. When you're both exhausted, there's nobody to call over.
  • It can feel lonely. Big moments happen and the people you want to share them with are far away.
  • It can bring up guilt or grief, that your baby won't grow up near their grandparents, or that you can't be there for your own parents as they get older.
  • Traditions can feel harder to keep, like the food, songs, language and celebrations you grew up with.

These feelings are normal, and you're far from the only dad who has them.

Letting family help from far away

Distance changes how family can help, but it doesn't stop them. Many relatives want a job. Give them one:

  • A regular video call, like Sunday mornings or bath time, so the baby gets to know their faces and voices.
  • Bedtime from afar. A grandparent can read a story, sing a song or say a prayer over video while you get the baby ready.
  • Recordings. Ask them to record lullabies, songs or stories from when you were small, in the language they speak at home.
  • Someone to talk to. Your dad, an uncle or a brother may have been through this. Ask them what the first months were like for them.
  • Planning a visit, even months away, gives everyone something to look forward to.

Building your people where you live

You may need to build some of your support from scratch. It takes time, but a few people can make a big difference:

  • Other parents nearby. Neighbors with kids, coworkers who are dads, families from daycare, the park or the library's baby story time.
  • Your faith or community group, if you have one. Many have families who will bring a meal or check in.
  • Family resource centers. Many towns have free centers with parent groups and classes. Call 211 or search 211.org and ask what's near you.
  • Other dads online. Free support groups for dads and our message board are there any hour.
  • Say yes when people offer. "Thanks, could you drop off dinner Thursday?" is a gift to them too.

See also feeling lonely as a new dad and family visiting after the baby.

Keeping your roots for your baby

You get to pass on where you come from. Speak your family's language at home if you have one. Cook the food you grew up with. Tell your baby stories about their grandparents. Play the music. Small things, done often, matter more than big ones.

When it's more than missing home

Missing family is normal. But if you've felt down, flat, angry or hopeless for more than two weeks, or you're pulling away from everyone, it may be more. Read the signs in dads and how to talk to your doctor. Help is free in English and Spanish at 988 (press 2 for Spanish) and 1-833-852-6262.

Common questions

Is it normal to feel lonely as a new dad when family is far away?

Yes. Many new dads miss the help and company of family who live in another state or country. Feeling lonely, sad or guilty about the distance is common.

How can grandparents help from far away?

Regular video calls, reading a bedtime story or singing over video, recording songs and stories, and simply being someone you can talk to all help, even from a distance.

Where can I find support near me if I don't have family here?

Try other parents nearby, a faith or community group, a family resource center, and free online groups for dads. Calling 211 is a good way to find local parent groups.

When is missing family more than homesickness?

If you've felt down, flat, angry or hopeless for more than two weeks, or you're pulling away from people, talk to a doctor. It may be depression, which is common and treatable.
