Your situation

After IVF or a long road to the baby

You waited, hoped and paid for this, maybe for years. Now the baby is here, and it’s harder than you expected. A lot of dads who went through IVF or fertility treatment feel this. You’re allowed to struggle.

If you’re thinking about suicide, please call or text 988 any time. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • You’re allowed to find this hard. Struggling doesn’t mean you’re ungrateful.
  • Say it to your partner: “This is harder than I thought. Is it for you too?”
  • Leave the big decisions, like trying again, for later.

Why it can hit after a long road

Getting here may have taken years. Appointments, shots, waiting, bad news, money, maybe losses along the way. That kind of stress doesn’t vanish the day the baby arrives. Sometimes it shows up after, once you finally stop holding your breath.

  • You were the steady one. A lot of dads hold it together through treatment and only feel it later.
  • You felt on the sidelines. Most of the procedures happened to your partner. Your stress often went unseen, even by you.
  • The money hurt. Treatment can drain savings right before a baby adds new costs. See money stress with a new baby.
  • The reality is hard. No sleep, a crying baby, no time. It can look nothing like the picture you held onto.

“We wanted this so much. Why do I feel this way?”

This is the guilt a lot of IVF dads carry. You fought for this baby, so you feel you have no right to struggle. But you do. Wanting a baby with all your heart doesn’t protect you from exhaustion, anxiety or depression. Feeling it doesn’t mean you’re ungrateful.

When you’re scared something will go wrong

After a long road, many parents find it hard to believe the baby is really here and really OK. You may check on them more, or brace for bad news. That’s understandable. If it keeps you from sleeping or fills your day, read anxious and on edge and safe sleep and the fear of SIDS.

If there were losses on the way

Failed cycles, transfers that didn’t take, or miscarriages are real losses. A new baby doesn’t cancel that grief. Both can be true at once. See grieving a pregnancy loss.

Your partner may feel it too

She went through a lot to get here. Many mothers after IVF feel the same guilt about struggling. Saying it out loud can help you both: “This is harder than I thought. Is it for you too?” See helping your partner.

Big decisions can wait

Whether to try again, and when, is a big question. It doesn’t have to be answered in the first year. Get through this part first.

Where to find support

  • RESOLVE, the national infertility association, runs peer support groups, many online. They also cover parenting after infertility.
  • A counselor who knows fertility. Ask your fertility clinic. Many have someone they refer to. See finding a therapist.
  • Other dads. PSI’s free dads’ groups meet online, and our message board is open any time.

If you used a donor or a surrogate, see gay dads and two-dad families and adoptive and foster dads too.

If it gets dark

If you’re thinking about suicide, or that your family would be better off without you, call or text 988. It’s free, 24/7, and you don’t have to be in crisis to call. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

Can dads get postpartum depression after IVF?

Yes. Wanting a baby badly doesn't protect you from exhaustion, anxiety or depression. Years of treatment stress can catch up with dads after the baby arrives.

Why do I feel guilty for struggling after IVF?

Many IVF parents feel they have no right to struggle after fighting so hard for their baby. But struggling doesn't mean you're ungrateful. It means you're human and tired.

Where can I find support after infertility?

RESOLVE, the national infertility association, runs peer support groups, many online. Your fertility clinic can often refer you to a counselor, and PSI has free online groups for dads.
