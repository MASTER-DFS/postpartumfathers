For dads

Start here: Postpartum depression in dads: signs, causes and help

Paternal postpartum depression usually shows up within the first year. It can look like anger, withdrawal, overwork, or numbness more than sadness.

What actually helps

  • Say it out loud. Tell your partner, a friend, or your doctor how you've really been.
  • Get checked. Your own doctor can screen you. Your partner's OB or your baby's pediatrician can point you to someone.
  • Talk to other dads. Join our message board, or try a free online group just for fathers from Postpartum Support International (PSI). See our community page.
  • Protect the basics. Sleep in shifts, eat real meals, get outside, and go easy on alcohol.
  • Use your benefits. Many employers offer a free, confidential Employee Assistance Program (EAP).

Supporting your partner

Start here: How to help your wife or partner with postpartum depression

  • Tell her what you're seeing, gently and without blame, and that you're in it together.
  • Help her book appointments and get to them. Offer to make the call.
  • Protect her sleep. Take a night feed or a solid block of hours.
  • Take things off her plate without waiting to be asked.
  • Know the warning signs of postpartum psychosis.
  • Look after yourself too. Dads whose partners are depressed are at higher risk themselves.

Finding a professional

  • PSI provider directory: perinatal mental health specialists near you.
  • FindTreatment.gov: SAMHSA's locator for mental health and substance use care.
  • Ask for a therapist with "perinatal" or "postpartum" experience. It's fine to say you're the dad. Therapy usually costs money; ask about insurance or a sliding scale.

Organizations

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Call, text, or chat any time you need to talk to someone.

988lifeline.org

Maternal Mental Health Hotline

24/7 call or text support for new parents and partners.

Call 1-833-852-6262

Postpartum Support International

HelpLine, free dad support groups, and a provider directory. The HelpLine is for support and referrals, not emergencies.

postpartum.net

Postpartum Men

Information and a self-assessment written for fathers.

postpartummen.com

Veterans Crisis Line

Dial 988 and press 1, text 838255, or chat online. Free, confidential and 24/7 for service members, veterans and their families. You don’t need to be enrolled in VA care.

veteranscrisisline.net

Poison Help

Free advice from medical specialists, 24/7, for questions as well as emergencies. If someone has collapsed, isn’t breathing or can’t be woken, call 911 instead.

Call 1-800-222-1222

211

Free, confidential, 24/7 help finding local support with rent, utilities, food, diapers, childcare and healthcare. Call 211, or search online.

211.org

HeadsUpGuys

Depression in men, not just after a baby: a free self-check, free self-guided courses, recovery stories from other men and a therapist directory.

headsupguys.org

Safe to Sleep

The National Institutes of Health campaign on safe infant sleep, with free materials and a section on infant loss and grief.

safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov

Programs for dads

  • PSI Help for Dads: free online support groups just for fathers, run by Postpartum Support International.
  • PSI Chats for Dads: a free live phone call with a licensed psychologist, the first Tuesday of every month at 5pm Pacific (8pm Eastern). Call 1-800-944-8766, code 73162#. Information, not therapy.
  • Sad Dads Club: a nonprofit for dads grieving the loss of a child, started by three fathers whose daughters were stillborn. Free peer support online and in person, and help reaching counseling at no cost to the family. It asks for donations and sells nothing.
  • Boot Camp for New Dads: a nonprofit workshop where experienced dads teach expecting dads what to expect. Use their locator to check for a class near you, or join their live online version. Fees vary.
  • Dads Supporting Dads: free to join. Local dad groups by city, plus groups for new dads, stay-at-home dads, sober dads, separated dads and dads of kids with disabilities. What’s running varies by area, so check what’s active near you.
  • Fatherhood.gov: the federal National Responsible Fatherhood Clearinghouse. Its program map finds fatherhood programs near you.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357: free, confidential, 24/7 referrals for mental health and drinking or drug use.
  • Paternal mental health fact sheet: a short research summary from the Maternal Mental Health Leadership Alliance. Handy to show a doctor.
  • MMHLA's full list of resources for fathers, for even more books, groups and podcasts.

Podcasts

Free episodes where dads talk about what they went through.

  • Dads Get Depressed Too: Beyond Postpartum, Pacific Post Partum Support Society. A father shares his depression after his son was born.
  • Healing From Paternal Postpartum Depression: Mom & Mind, with Kevin Seldon.
  • Paternal Postpartum Depression: Mom & Mind, with Dr David Levine, a pediatrician who went through it himself after both his children.
  • Supporting Dads and Their Mental Health in the NICU: NICU Heroes, Hand to Hold, a nonprofit for NICU families. Three dads talk about how the NICU affected their own mental health, and how to look after your partner and yourself during a stay.

Books

These cost money to buy, but most libraries carry them or can order them for you for free.

  • Daddy Blues: Postnatal Depression and Fatherhood by Mark Williams. One father's own story.
  • DAD: Untold Stories of Fatherhood, Love, Mental Health and Masculinity by Elliott Rae. Twenty fathers in their own words.
  • The Postpartum Husband by Karen Kleiman. A practical guide for supporting a partner with postpartum depression.
  • Fathers and Perinatal Mental Health by Jane Hanley and Mark Williams. More detailed, written for professionals and families.

Know a free resource that helped you? Email info@postpartumfathers.org and we'll look at adding it.

Outside the US

Most of this page is for the US. If you’re somewhere else, findahelpline.com lists free crisis lines by country. A few places that support dads:

  • DadSpace (Australia): resources for expecting and new dads from the Parent-Infant Research Institute, including DadBooster, a free online program for dads dealing with stress, anxiety or low mood. For dads in Australia.
  • PANDAS Foundation, Support for Dads (UK): a monthly support call for dads and non-birthing parents, a free bookable call service, WhatsApp support and groups.
  • Pacific Post Partum Support Society, For Partners (British Columbia, Canada): phone and text support for partners. Call or text 604-255-7999, or call toll-free 1-855-255-7999.

Print and share

At the hospital and at the two-week checkup, the mom gets handed a stack of postpartum information and the dad usually gets nothing. One page on a noticeboard changes that for somebody.

A one-page flyer you can print

Black and white, one sheet, with a QR code to this site and the crisis numbers on it. Print a few and leave them at your pediatrician’s office, your OB’s office, a birth class or a break room.
