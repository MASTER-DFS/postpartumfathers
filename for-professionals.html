For people who work with new families

For the people who see new dads

Nurses, pediatricians, OBs, midwives, doulas, lactation consultants, therapists, clergy. You may be the one person outside the family who sees a new dad up close. This page explains, in a minute, a free place you can point him to.

What this site is

  • Short, free guides for new dads, written in plain words for a tired dad reading on his phone in the middle of the night.
  • Every guide is in English and Spanish.
  • No ads, nothing for sale, no affiliate links, no tracking. Nobody has to sign up to read the guides.
  • A free message board where dads talk with other dads (free sign-up, in English).
  • Crisis numbers at the top of every page.

A way to mention it

You don’t need a script. Something like this is enough:

“How are you holding up? A lot of new dads have a harder time than they let on. There’s a free site for dads called postpartumfathers.org.”

In Spanish: “¿Y tú cómo estás? Hay un sitio gratis para papás, en español: postpartumfathers.org/es”

If a mom or a family member is the one worried about him, there’s a page for them too: Worried about a new dad?

Why dads

About 1 in 10 new fathers become depressed during pregnancy or the first year after birth, and the risk is higher when his partner has postpartum depression. In dads it often looks like a short temper, pulling away, working late, drinking more, or feeling numb, so it gets missed. Most postpartum information goes to the mom, and few people ask the dad how he’s doing.

If a dad tells you he’s thinking about suicide, or you’re worried about his safety, follow your own workplace’s steps first. He can call or text 988 any time, day or night (press 2 for Spanish). The National Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, is free, 24/7, call or text, in English and Spanish, and dads can call too. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911. The Postpartum Support International HelpLine, 1-800-944-4773, offers support and referrals but is not a crisis line.

Who’s behind it, and what it isn’t

Postpartum Fathers is a volunteer project run by David, a dad. Postpartum Support International lists this site on its Help for Dads page. He isn’t a doctor or a therapist, and nothing here has been reviewed by a clinician. Most of the facts come from Postpartum Support International, the American Academy of Pediatrics and the National Institutes of Health, and the guides link to them. It’s peer support, not care, and it doesn’t replace a doctor or a licensed mental health professional.

If you spot something wrong or out of date, please email info@postpartumfathers.org and it gets fixed. Please don’t send anyone’s personal or health details.

Last updated November 2026. Source for “1 in 10”: Paulson JF, Bazemore SD. Prenatal and postpartum depression in fathers and its association with maternal depression: a meta-analysis. JAMA. 2010;303(19):1961-1969.
