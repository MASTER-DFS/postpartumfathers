How you're feeling

It's been a year and I still feel this way

Most of what you read about new dads stops at the first few months. But your kid is walking, everyone thinks you're past it, and you still feel flat, tense, or not like yourself. That's more common than people say, and help still works, however long it's been.

If you're ever thinking about suicide or feel your family would be better off without you, please call or text 988 any time, day or night. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911. More crisis help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Know that there's no deadline. Feeling this way a year or two in still counts.
  • Book one appointment with your doctor and say how long it's been.
  • Tell one person how you've really been doing.

Why it can last, or start later

Depression in dads doesn't follow a calendar. For some it starts in the first months and never fully lifts. For others it creeps in later, once the help from family stops, leave is long over, and the sleep debt has been piling up for a year.

The toddler years bring their own load: tantrums, daycare bugs, a second baby, a job that expects you back at full speed. Many dads also tell themselves they should be over it by now, which makes it harder to say anything.

What it can look like now

  • Flat or numb most days
  • Short temper that won't let up
  • Going through the motions with your kid
  • Working late or staying busy to avoid home
  • More drinking, gaming or scrolling
  • Tired all the time, even after sleep
  • Pulling away from your partner and friends
  • Feeling like your family would be better off without you

If several of these have lasted more than two weeks, it's worth talking to someone. If you've thought your family would be better off without you, that one is serious. Please call or text 988 now. For a few honest questions, see is this more than tired?

You don't need the word "postpartum"

You don't have to prove when it started, or fit a label, to get help. A doctor only needs to hear how you've been. Try: "Since my kid was born I haven't felt like myself, and it's been over a year. I'm angry or flat most days." See how to talk to your doctor.

Depression is very treatable at any point. Talk therapy, medicine, or both help most people feel better. See finding a therapist, and paying for it.

Small steps that help

  • Get one thing back that's yours. A game with friends, the gym, a hobby. It isn't selfish.
  • Find something you enjoy with your kid. Toddlers are often easier to connect with than babies. See things to do with your baby.
  • Look at sleep again. If you still haven't slept properly in a year, that alone needs fixing. See can't sleep even when the baby does.
  • Talk to other dads. Many are a year or more in too. Try free support groups for dads or our message board.

It can feel like you've missed your chance to get help. You haven't. Your kid will need you for a long time, and feeling better now matters for both of you.

Common questions

Can postpartum depression in dads last more than a year?

Yes. Depression in fathers can last past the first year, or start later, often in the toddler years. It is still treatable however long it has been.

Is it too late to get help if my child is already a toddler?

No. You do not need to prove when it started or fit a label. Tell a doctor how long you have felt this way. Therapy, medicine or both help most people.

What does depression look like in a dad with a toddler?

Feeling flat or numb, a short temper that will not let up, going through the motions, staying busy to avoid home, and drinking or scrolling more.

Where can I talk to other dads who feel this way?

Free online groups for fathers and our message board are open to dads at any stage. If you are in crisis, call or text 988.
