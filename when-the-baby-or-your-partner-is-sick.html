When someone is sick

When the baby or your partner is sick

Some weeks, everything lands on you. The baby has a fever. Your partner is still healing from a hard birth, or is sick, or is struggling in a way you can't fix. Suddenly you're the nurse, the cook, the night shift and the one making calls. Here's what helps, and when to get help fast.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Write down the doctor's after-hours number and the nurse line on your insurance card.
  • Cancel everything that isn't essential this week.
  • Ask one person for one specific thing: a meal, a pharmacy run, or a few hours of sleep.

When the baby is sick

A sick baby is scary, especially the first time. Babies can't tell you what's wrong, so you go on how they look, eat and act. Trust your gut. If something seems off, call the doctor's office, even at night. Calling about a small worry is what they're there for.

For a baby under 3 months, any fever is a reason to call right away. The first week home lists the other signs to call about. Call 911 if your baby is struggling to breathe, turning blue, or hard to wake.

For crying that won't stop, see colic and reflux.

When your partner is recovering from birth

Healing from birth takes weeks, longer after a C-section or a hard delivery. During that time, your job is to take everything else off their plate so they can rest. That includes the house, the meals, visitors and night duty as much as you can.

Know the warning signs. The CDC says to get medical care right away for any of these, up to a year after birth:

  • A headache that won't go away or gets worse, or changes in vision
  • Dizziness or fainting
  • A fever of 100.4°F or higher
  • Trouble breathing, chest pain, or a fast-beating heart
  • Severe nausea and throwing up
  • Severe belly pain that doesn't go away
  • Extreme swelling of the hands or face
  • Swelling, redness or pain in a leg or arm
  • Heavy bleeding or discharge
  • Overwhelming tiredness
  • Thoughts of harming themselves or the baby

Go with them, or call 911 if it can't wait. Say they recently gave birth.

When your partner is struggling with their mental health

Some illnesses don't show on the outside. If your partner seems deeply sad, anxious, numb or not themselves, read helping your partner with postpartum depression.

When everyone is sick at once

Sooner or later, the whole house gets the same bug. Survival mode is allowed. Paper plates, cartoons, cereal for dinner, and the house a mess. Wash hands often, keep fluids going for everyone, and let the rest wait.

Carrying it all alone

When your partner is down, you may be running the whole house and still going to work. That's a lot, and it often comes with worry you don't say out loud. Keep yourself fed and get sleep wherever you can, because the house runs on you right now.

Ask for help before you hit empty. Tell work what's going on if you can. If you're feeling overwhelmed, reach out:

  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, free and 24/7, dads too.
  • Our message board, open any time.
  • PSI's free dads' groups, which meet online.

Common questions

When should I call the doctor about my sick baby?

Trust your gut and call if something seems off, even at night. For a baby under 3 months, any fever is a reason to call right away. Call 911 if your baby is struggling to breathe, turning blue or hard to wake.

What are the warning signs after birth?

The CDC says to get medical care right away for signs like a headache that won't go away, vision changes, fever of 100.4°F or higher, trouble breathing, chest pain, severe belly pain, leg swelling or pain, heavy bleeding, or thoughts of self-harm.

How can I help my partner recover after a hard birth?

Take everything else off their plate so they can rest: the house, meals, visitors and as much of the night as you can. Know the warning signs and get care fast if you see them.

How do I cope when I'm carrying everything alone?

Cut back to essentials, keep yourself fed and sleep when you can. Ask for specific help early, and call the Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262 if you're overwhelmed.
