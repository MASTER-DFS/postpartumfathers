Signs it's more than tired

Using weed or pills to cope

A joint to sleep. An edible to quiet your head. A pill to get through the day. A lot of new dads lean on something. No judgment here. Here’s how to keep your baby safe, and how to find something that works better.

If someone has taken too much and is hard to wake or not breathing well, call 911. If you’re thinking about suicide, call or text 988.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • If you’ve used, hand the baby to a sober adult. Never sleep with them after using.
  • Lock up edibles and pills where kids can’t reach.
  • Ask yourself what it’s helping with: sleep, worry, or feeling numb? That’s what needs help.

You’re not the only one

A lot of new dads reach for something to take the edge off. A joint to fall asleep. An edible to stop the racing thoughts. Leftover pain pills, or someone else’s anxiety meds. Whether it’s legal where you live or not, this page isn’t here to judge. It’s here to help you stay safe and get what you actually need.

Signs it’s becoming a problem

  • You need it to sleep, or to face the evening
  • You’re using more, or more often, than before the baby
  • You use alone, or hide how much
  • You’ve tried to cut back and it didn’t stick
  • You’re using while you’re the one in charge of the baby

If a couple of these fit, it’s worth a closer look. It’s often a sign that something underneath, like anxiety, depression or no sleep, needs help.

Keeping the baby safe

  • Never sleep with the baby, or hold them where you might doze off, after using anything that makes you drowsy. See safe sleep.
  • Have a sober adult on duty. If you’ve used, hand the baby over.
  • No smoke or vapor around the baby, in the house or the car.
  • Lock up edibles and pills. Edibles look like candy to kids. If a child swallows any, call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222, or 911 if they’re hard to wake or having trouble breathing.

A word on pills

  • Pills that don’t come from a pharmacy can contain fentanyl, even if they look real. A tiny amount can kill.
  • Keep naloxone (Narcan) at home if anyone in the house uses opioids. It’s sold over the counter at most pharmacies.
  • Don’t stop prescription anxiety meds or pain pills suddenly if you’ve been taking them a while. Withdrawal can be dangerous, and stopping some anxiety or sleep meds (like Xanax, Klonopin or Ativan) all at once can cause seizures. The same goes for heavy daily drinking. Talk to a doctor about tapering.
  • Never mix pain pills or anxiety meds with alcohol. Together they can slow your breathing, even at normal doses, and that can kill.

What to try instead

The thing you’re using is doing a job, like helping you sleep or calming you down. Find another way to do that job.

  • Can’t sleep? See can’t sleep even when the baby does.
  • Wound up and anxious? See anxious and on edge.
  • Numb and flat? See the signs of depression in dads.
  • Drinking too? See drinking more since the baby.

Getting help

The SAMHSA National Helpline is free, confidential and open 24/7 in English and Spanish: 1-800-662-4357. They’ll point you to treatment and support near you, insurance or not. You can also search FindTreatment.gov. Your own doctor is a good place to start too. See how to talk to your doctor.

If it gets dark

If you’re thinking about suicide, call or text 988, free and 24/7. If someone has taken too much and is hard to wake, breathing slowly or not at all, call 911 and give naloxone if you have it.

Common questions

Is it OK to use weed to cope with a new baby?

A lot of new dads do, but it's worth a closer look if you need it to sleep, use more than before, or use while you're in charge of the baby. Never sleep with the baby or hold them where you might doze off after using, and keep edibles locked away.

Where can I get help for weed or pills?

The SAMHSA National Helpline is free, confidential and 24/7 in English and Spanish at 1-800-662-4357. You can also search FindTreatment.gov or talk to your doctor.

What if my child eats an edible?

Call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222. Call 911 if the child is hard to wake or having trouble breathing.
