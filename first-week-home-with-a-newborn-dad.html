Week one

The first week home with a newborn: a dad’s guide

You're home, the door closes, and suddenly it's just you, your family and this tiny person. Most dads feel some mix of love, panic and exhaustion that first week. That's normal. Nobody knows what they're doing at first. Here's what matters most, and what can wait.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

The safety basics

  • Safe sleep, every time: baby sleeps alone, on their back, in a crib or bassinet with a firm, flat mattress and nothing else in it. No pillows, blankets or bumpers.
  • Never fall asleep with the baby on a couch or armchair. If you're nodding off, put them down in their crib.
  • Car seat: rear-facing, straps snug, every ride.
  • Never shake a baby. If the crying gets to you, put the baby down safely and step away. Read when the baby won't stop crying.

When to call the doctor

Call your baby's doctor right away for a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher, trouble breathing, not feeding, very few wet diapers, skin or eyes turning yellow, or if your baby seems unusually sleepy or floppy. If something feels wrong, call. In an emergency, call 911.

How dads can help most

  • Be the gatekeeper. Keep visits short, or ask people to wait a week.
  • Handle the house. Meals, dishes, laundry, errands.
  • Take a night shift if you can, so everyone gets one real stretch of sleep.
  • Own the diapers and burping. It's hands-on time, and it helps you bond.
  • Say yes to help with food and errands.

Don't forget you

Eat, drink water, and grab sleep when you can. It's normal to feel overwhelmed in week one. If low mood, worry or numbness hang around more than two weeks, read the signs of postpartum depression in dads and anxiety in new dads. You've got this.

Common questions

What should dads do the first week home with a newborn?

Follow safe sleep every time, handle meals and the house, keep visits short, take a night shift if you can, and get hands-on with diapers and burping.

What are the safe sleep rules for a newborn?

Baby sleeps alone, on their back, in a crib or bassinet with a firm, flat mattress and nothing else in it. Never fall asleep with the baby on a couch or chair.

When should I call the doctor about my newborn?

Call right away for a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher, trouble breathing, not feeding, very few wet diapers, yellow skin or eyes, or unusual sleepiness.
