Your situation

When the baby wasn’t planned

Maybe you weren’t trying. Maybe you weren’t sure you wanted kids, or not yet. Now a baby is coming, or already here. Whatever you’re feeling, a lot of good dads started exactly where you are.

If you feel trapped or think about suicide, please call or text 988 any time. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Let yourself feel it. Shock, fear and guilt are all normal. They don’t decide what kind of dad you’ll be.
  • Pick one next step, not ten. Just the next appointment, or one look at the money.
  • Tell one person you trust how you really feel.

What a lot of dads feel

An unplanned baby can bring up a lot at once. All of it is normal.

  • Shock. Your plans just changed overnight.
  • Fear. About money, about being ready, about doing it right.
  • Guilt for not feeling excited, when everyone expects you to be.
  • Grief for the life you thought you’d have this year.
  • Numbness. It may not feel real until the baby is here, or even after.

None of this makes you a bad dad. Feelings don’t decide what kind of father you’ll be. What you do does.

Love can come later

Lots of dads don’t feel a big rush of love right away, planned or not. For many, it grows slowly through the feeds, the diapers and the late nights. Showing up every day is how it starts. See not feeling bonded yet.

One step at a time

You don’t have to have it all figured out. Pick the next thing.

  • Get to the appointments if you can. Hearing the heartbeat or seeing the scan makes it real for a lot of dads.
  • Look at the money once, on paper. It’s less scary than the version in your head. See money stress with a new baby.
  • Check your job’s leave. See taking paternity leave.
  • Learn the basics. Feeding, diapers, safe sleep. See expecting a baby and the first week home.

Whatever your situation

Maybe you’re together. Maybe you’re not. Maybe it’s still new. Whatever it is, your baby can still have you in their life. These may help:

  • Young dads
  • Not living with your baby
  • Raising your baby on your own
  • Going from one kid to two, if this is baby number two or more

Talk to someone

Keeping it all inside makes it heavier. Talk to a friend, your own dad or another dad you trust. PSI’s free dads’ groups meet online, and our message board is open any time.

If the shock turns into feeling low most days for more than two weeks, that may be depression, and it can start before the baby is even born. Read is this more than tired?

If it gets dark

If you feel trapped, can’t see a way forward, or think about suicide, call or text 988. It’s free, 24/7, and you don’t have to be in crisis to call. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

Is it normal to not be excited about an unplanned baby?

Yes. Shock, fear, guilt and numbness are all common. Feelings don't decide what kind of dad you'll be. For many dads, love grows slowly through showing up every day.

How do I prepare for a baby I didn't plan for?

Take it one step at a time. Go to the appointments if you can, look at the money once on paper, check your job's leave, and learn the basics like feeding and safe sleep.

What if we're not together?

Your baby can still have you in their life. See our guides on not living with your baby, young dads and raising your baby on your own.
