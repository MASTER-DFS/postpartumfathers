Dad and baby

Things to do with your baby as a dad

Look for baby activities and almost everything is called "mommy and me." It can seem like nothing out there is for dads. There's more than it looks like. Most of those classes welcome any parent, and plenty of good things cost nothing at all.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Look up your library's baby story time. It's free, and any parent can go.
  • Plan one outing a week, just you and the baby. Same day, same time.
  • Ask "are dads welcome?" before you skip a "mommy and me" class. The answer is usually yes.

"Mommy and me" usually means any parent

Many classes use that name out of habit. A quick call or message asking whether fathers can join usually gets a yes.

You might be the only dad at first. That's okay. Other dads often start coming once they see one there.

Free things to do together

  • Library baby story time. Babies get songs, rhymes and bouncing, and you get a room of other parents. Free at most public libraries. Look for "baby" or "lapsit" on the events calendar.
  • Parks and trails. Baby in a carrier or stroller, a different route each week.
  • Everyday errands. The hardware store, the farmers market, a coffee pickup. Babies love new sights and sounds.
  • Museums and zoos. Many have free days or free entry for babies. Check before you go.

Find other dads

  • Dads Supporting Dads has local dad groups in some cities, with family play dates and dads' nights out, plus groups online. See if there's one near you.
  • Boot Camp for New Dads is a workshop for dads-to-be, taught by recent dads who bring their babies. Once yours is here, you can go back as one of those dads, baby in tow. There's usually a fee.
  • Your parks and recreation department or community center may list parent and baby programs. Ask if they have anything for dads.

For tips on actually starting a conversation with another dad, read lonely as a new dad.

Paid classes worth a look

These cost money. Ask about prices and whether you can try one class first.

  • Parent and baby swim classes, usually from about 6 months. More in bonding with your baby.
  • Baby music classes. Singing, shakers and bouncing. Babies don't care if you can sing.
  • Parent and baby classes at a local community center, often by age group.

Ideas for home

  • Tummy time on your chest. Lie back and put the baby on your chest, face to face. Tummy time is only while the baby is awake and you're watching. If you feel sleepy, move the baby to their crib. See Safe to Sleep.
  • Read anything. Board books, the sports page, a recipe. Your voice is what matters.
  • Music and dancing. Put on your music and sway around the kitchen together.
  • A mirror. Babies love looking at faces, including their own.
  • Show them your world. Walk them around the yard or the garage and name what they see. Keep them away from tools, heat and anything sharp.

Start a small tradition

Pick one thing that's yours and the baby's. A Saturday morning walk, the same coffee shop, a photo in the same chair each month. Small traditions add up, and they grow with your child.

Common questions

What can dads do with a newborn?

Free library baby story times, walks with a carrier or stroller, errands, tummy time on your chest while you're awake, reading, music, and a small weekly tradition.

Are mommy and me classes open to dads?

Usually, yes. Many classes use that name out of habit. Call ahead and ask if dads are welcome.

Are there groups for dads with babies?

Yes. Dads Supporting Dads has local groups in some cities and online, Boot Camp for New Dads lets recent dads come back with their babies, and parks and recreation departments often list parent and baby programs.

Are baby classes free?

Many library story times are free. Swim, music and community center classes usually cost money, so ask about prices first.
