Holidays with a new baby

The first holidays with a baby are supposed to be magical. In real life they often mean long drives, packed houses, skipped naps and a dozen opinions about everything. You may feel pulled between family, your partner and a baby who just wants to sleep. You're allowed to keep it small this year. A calm holiday beats a perfect one.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Decide together, before anyone calls, where you'll go and how long you'll stay.
  • Pick a leave time and tell the hosts ahead of time, so leaving isn't a fight.
  • Pack a quiet plan: a room or the car where the baby can nap and you can breathe.

You can say no to some of it

There may be three families expecting you on the same day. You can skip some. Choose what matters most and say the rest kindly: "We're keeping it simple this year with the baby. We'd love to see you after." Most people understand once they hear it plainly.

If saying no is hard for you, saying no as a new dad has more words you can use.

Be the one who guards the baby's routine

Naps and feeds don't take holidays off, and an overtired baby makes the whole day harder. Many dads find it useful to own this job. Watch the clock, step away for feeds and naps, and be the one who says "time for a break." It takes the pressure off your partner, too.

  • Hand the baby around less than people want. It's fine to say "they're just about to sleep."
  • Ask anyone who's sick to skip holding the baby. Colds and flu spread fast at gatherings. Ask your baby's doctor which vaccines visitors should have.
  • Bring a portable crib. Baby on their back, crib bare. Couches, adult beds and car seats out of the car aren't safe for sleep. See Safe to Sleep.

Travel with a baby

Long trips go better in short legs. Plan stops every couple of hours for feeds and diapers. Leave around nap time if your baby sleeps in the car. Pack one bag that holds everything for the next few hours, so you're not digging through the trunk. For crying on the road, see baby crying in the car.

When family gets to you

Holidays can stir up old family stuff. Unwanted advice, comments about how you're doing things, or old tension coming back. Agree with your partner ahead of time that each of you handles your own family. If you need to cool off, get some air on the porch. Then come back in.

For more on relatives and advice, read family visiting after the baby.

If the season feels heavy

Not everyone feels festive. Holidays can bring up grief, money stress or loneliness, all on top of no sleep. If you're dreading it or feeling low, plenty of dads feel the same.

  • Our message board, open any time, including holidays.
  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, free and 24/7, dads too.
  • 988, call or text, every day of the year.

Common questions

How do we handle holidays with a newborn?

Keep it small. Decide together where you'll go and for how long, tell hosts your leave time ahead, protect naps and feeds, and plan a quiet spot for breaks.

How do I say no to family over the holidays?

Say it simply and kindly: "We're keeping it simple this year with the baby. We'd love to see you after." You don't need to give a long reason.

Where should a baby sleep when we travel?

In a portable crib or bassinet, on their back, with nothing else in it. Couches, adult beds and car seats out of the car aren't safe places for a baby to sleep.

What if the holidays make me feel worse?

That's common, especially with grief, money stress or little sleep. Talk to someone. The Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262 and 988 are open every day of the year.
