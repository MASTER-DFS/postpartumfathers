Stay-at-home dads

Stay-at-home dad with a newborn? You’re not alone

More dads are staying home with their babies than ever. It can be one of the best things you ever do, and some days it can also feel like the loneliest job in the world. If you're finding it hard, that doesn't mean you're not cut out for it. It means it's a real job, and you deserve support.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

What can be hard

  • Isolation. Long days with no adult conversation.
  • Feeling out of place at baby groups or the playground where it's mostly moms.
  • Losing part of who you were, especially if work was a big part of that.
  • No clock-out time. The job doesn't end when someone else gets home.

What helps

  • Build a loose routine. Walk, nap, lunch, outing. Having a shape to the day helps you both.
  • Get out every day. The park, the library, the grocery store. Fresh air and other people help.
  • Go to the groups anyway. Many library story times and parent groups welcome dads, and you might be the reason the next dad feels welcome.
  • Find other at-home dads. Search for local dad groups, or ask at your library or community center.
  • Keep something that's yours. A workout, a hobby, a weekly night out. Protect it.
  • Agree on breaks at home. Time off when another adult is around matters for you too.

When it gets heavy

If you feel stuck, numb, or hopeless for more than two weeks, that can be depression, and it's common in dads. Read feeling lonely as a new dad, the signs of postpartum depression in dads and how to talk to your doctor. You can talk to other fathers any time on our message board or at PSI's free dads' groups.

Common questions

Is it normal for stay-at-home dads to feel isolated?

Yes. Long days without adult conversation and feeling out of place at parent groups are common. Getting out daily and finding other dads helps.

How do stay-at-home dads find other dads?

Ask at libraries and community centers, search for local dad groups, and try online groups like PSI's free dads' groups.

How can stay-at-home dads avoid burnout?

Keep a loose routine, get out every day, protect time for yourself, and agree on regular breaks when another adult is home.
