Grief and a new baby

Losing a parent around the time your baby arrives

Sometimes life hands you the biggest beginning and the biggest ending at once. A parent dies, or gets very sick, in the same months your baby arrives. You may be planning a funeral with a newborn in the house, or holding your baby and wishing your mom or dad could see them. There's no right way to feel this. Both things are true.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Let yourself cry if it comes. Holding it in for everyone else wears you down.
  • Hand off one task to someone who offered to help: meals, calls or paperwork.
  • Say their name to your baby. It's a small way to keep them close.

Joy and grief, side by side

You might laugh at your baby's first smile and then feel the loss hit a minute later. Some dads feel guilty for being happy. Others feel numb and worry that something's wrong with them. Grief doesn't follow a schedule, and a new baby doesn't pause it. Both feelings can share the same day.

Little time to grieve

With a newborn, there's no room to stop. You may push grief aside to get through feeds and work. That's often what it takes in the moment. But grief that's never let out tends to come back later. Try to give it a little space, even ten minutes a day, a walk alone, or a talk with a sibling.

When you're the one handling everything

A death brings calls, paperwork, a service and family to manage. Share the load. Siblings, cousins and friends often want a job. If a hospice was involved, ask about their family support. Many hospices offer grief support to families after a death. Let the rest wait if it can.

If you're also caring for a sick parent while the baby is new, be honest with work about what's going on. Work stress and a new baby may help.

Keeping their memory in your child's life

Your baby won't remember your parent, but they can still know them. Keep photos up. Tell stories as your child grows. Cook their recipes, play their music, and use their name. Some dads write a letter to their child about the grandparent they never met. It can help you grieve, too.

When grief gets heavier

If, after a few weeks, you can't function, feel hopeless, or start drinking more to cope, get support. Grief and a new baby together can tip into depression. Read depression in new dads for the signs.

  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, free and 24/7, dads too.
  • Our message board, open any time.
  • PSI's free dads' groups, which meet online.
  • 988, call or text, if you're having thoughts of not wanting to be here.

Common questions

How do I grieve a parent with a newborn at home?

Give grief a little space each day, even a short walk alone or a talk with a sibling. Share the tasks, accept help, and let what can wait, wait.

Is it normal to feel happy and sad at the same time?

Yes. Joy about your baby and grief for your parent can share the same day, even the same minute. Neither feeling cancels the other.

How can I keep my parent's memory alive for my baby?

Keep photos up, tell stories as your child grows, use their name, cook their recipes, and consider writing your child a letter about them.

When is grief a sign I need help?

If after a few weeks you can't function, feel hopeless, or are drinking more to cope, talk to someone. The Maternal Mental Health Hotline is 1-833-852-6262, and 988 is there for thoughts of not wanting to be here.
