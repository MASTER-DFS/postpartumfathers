Young dads

Maybe you're still in school, or just starting work. Maybe people looked worried when they heard the news. Becoming a dad young comes with extra pressure, and sometimes with people who expect you to fail. You don't have to prove them right. What your child needs most from you isn't money or age. It's you, showing up.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • Show up for one thing this week. A feed, a bath, a checkup, a walk.
  • Tell one adult you trust what you're worried about, whether that's a parent, coach, teacher or pastor.
  • Call 211 to ask what help for young parents exists where you live.

Being there counts most

Kids whose dads stay involved tend to do better, and that's true at any age. Nobody expects you to have it all figured out. Holding your baby, feeding them, changing diapers and being around on a regular schedule all matter more than gifts or money.

If you don't live with your baby, read dads who don't live with their baby.

Keep your own future going

It can feel like you have to drop everything and just work. Try not to give up on school or training if you can avoid it. Finishing it often leads to steadier work later, which helps your child too.

Ask your school counselor about options for young parents. Many schools and colleges have them, from flexible schedules to help with child care.

If you're in school, use its health services too. High schools have counselors and often a nurse. Community colleges and universities often run a health center and counseling for enrolled students, usually free or low cost. You can go there for stress, sleep, mood or anything else that's weighing on you.

Your legal rights

If you're not married to your baby's mom, you may not be the legal father yet, even if everyone knows you are. Hospitals and child support offices can explain how to make it official, often by signing a form. Being the legal dad matters for visits, decisions and your child's benefits later.

If things with your baby's mom are rocky, keep it about the baby. Stay calm and stay consistent.

Help with money and basics

  • 211 connects you to local help with food, housing, jobs and child care.
  • WIC can help with food and formula for your baby, if you qualify. Ask at your baby's doctor's office.
  • Fatherhood.gov has free tips and ways to find fatherhood programs.

For more on bills and stress, read money stress with a new baby.

It's OK to not be OK

Young dads deal with a lot at once, so feeling scared, angry or low comes with the territory. If it won't lift, talk to someone.

  • Our message board, open any time.
  • PSI's free dads' groups, which meet online.
  • Your school or college counseling center, if you're a student.
  • 988, call or text, any time, about anything that feels like too much.

Common questions

Can I be a good dad if I'm young?

Yes. What your child needs most is you showing up regularly: feeding, holding, changing and spending time with them. That matters more than money or age.

Should I drop out of school to support my baby?

Try to keep going if you can. Finishing school or training often leads to steadier work later. Ask your school counselor what options exist for young parents.

Am I the legal father if we're not married?

Not always. If you're not married to your baby's mom, ask the hospital or your local child support office how to make it official. It often means signing a form.

Where can young dads get help?

If you're a student, start with your school or college health and counseling services. Call 211 for local help with food, housing, jobs and child care. WIC may help with food and formula. Fatherhood.gov lists free tips and fatherhood programs.
