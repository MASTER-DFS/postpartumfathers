The mental load

Carrying the house in your head

Diapers are low. The doctor's appointment needs moving. Someone needs a ride, a bill is due, and the texts keep coming while you're trying to work. Keeping track of all of it is a job of its own, even when nobody sees it. If your head never gets quiet, here's how to carry less of it.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Why it wears you down

Remembering, planning, and answering questions all day uses the same energy as your actual work. You might feel tired even on a day you didn't do much, or find it hard to switch off at night because your brain is still running the list. That's normal. It's a sign you're carrying a lot, not a sign you're failing.

Get it out of your head

  • One list, not a hundred thoughts. Keep a single running list on your phone or the fridge. When something pops into your head, write it down and let it go.
  • Make it shared. A shared list or calendar with the other adults in the house means nobody has to be the only one who remembers.
  • Put repeat stuff on autopilot. Auto-pay bills, recurring orders for diapers and wipes, and reminders for things like refills.
  • Same meals, same days. A simple weekly meal plan saves a lot of daily deciding.

When the texts keep coming at work

  • Pick check-in times. Look at home messages at set times, like mid-morning, lunch, and mid-afternoon, unless it's urgent.
  • Agree on what "urgent" means. A call for emergencies, texts for everything else.
  • Batch the small stuff. "Add it to the list and I'll handle it at lunch" keeps things from pulling you away every ten minutes.
  • Answer once. Questions that come up again and again can live on the fridge: the doctor's number, the bedtime routine, where the spare diapers are.

When you're away

Before a trip, leave the essentials written down, set up deliveries, and agree on a daily call time. Then trust that things will be okay without you managing them from a hotel room. More ideas are in traveling for work with a new baby at home.

Give yourself an off switch

  • End the day with a brain dump. Write down tomorrow's three most important things, then close the list.
  • Not everything has to be done well. Fed, safe, and loved is the bar. Some things can wait.
  • Hand off whole jobs, not just tasks, when you can, so someone else owns it from start to finish.

If your mind won't stop

If racing thoughts keep you up at night or the worry won't let go, read anxiety in new dads and can't sleep even when the baby sleeps. If you're running on empty, read worn down as a new dad and working from home with a newborn. Other dads get it too: PSI's free dads' groups meet online.

Common questions

What is the mental load for dads?

It's the invisible work of remembering, planning, and keeping track of the household, like appointments, supplies, bills, and rides. It can be tiring even on days you don't do much.

How do I stop thinking about home stuff while I work?

Keep one shared list, check home messages at set times, agree that urgent things come by phone call, and batch small requests for a break.

How can I carry less of the household to-do list?

Get it out of your head onto a shared list or calendar, automate repeat things like bills and diaper orders, and hand off whole jobs so someone else owns them start to finish.

What if I can't switch off at night?

Try a short brain dump before bed: write tomorrow's top three things and close the list. If racing thoughts keep you up for weeks, talk to a doctor.
