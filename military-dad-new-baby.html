Military and veteran dads

Military dad with a new baby? There’s support for you

Long hours, training, deployments, moves. Military life puts extra weight on new dads, and the culture can make it hard to admit you're struggling. Asking for support is part of looking after your family and your mission. There's free, confidential help made for you.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

What can make it harder

  • Missing the birth or early weeks because of duty or deployment.
  • Coming home to a family that's found a rhythm without you.
  • Moving away from family and friends who could help.
  • Worry about how asking for help looks.

Free, confidential help

  • Military OneSource: 800-342-9647. Free, confidential support for service members and families, including non-medical counseling.
  • Veterans Crisis Line: call 988 and press 1, or text 838255. For veterans, service members and their families, 24/7.
  • The Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262, takes calls and texts from partners and family too, 24/7.
  • Your installation's family support center often runs new parent programs, including some for dads.

Staying close from far away

  • Record yourself reading a book or singing a song so the baby can hear your voice.
  • Video call when you can, even for a few minutes.
  • When you're home, get hands-on right away. Read simple ways to bond as a dad.

If you're feeling on edge, numb or hopeless, read the signs of postpartum depression in dads and how to talk to your doctor. Other fathers get it: PSI's free dads' groups meet online, wherever you're stationed.

Common questions

Where can military dads get mental health support?

Military OneSource at 800-342-9647 offers free, confidential support. The Veterans Crisis Line is 988, then press 1, or text 838255.

Can military dads get postpartum depression?

Yes. Any father can, and deployments, moves and long hours can add to the strain. It is common and treatable.

How can deployed dads bond with a new baby?

Record yourself reading or singing, video call when possible, and get hands-on with care as soon as you're home.
