Lost your job with a new baby

Getting laid off with a newborn at home is one of the scariest things a dad can face. You’re not a failure, and you’re not alone. Here’s what to do first, one step at a time.

If you’re thinking about suicide, please call or text 988 any time. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • You don’t have to fix it all tonight. Just write down the first three things to do tomorrow.
  • Tomorrow, file for unemployment first. Then health insurance.
  • Tell your partner, and tell her you’ve already got a plan started.

First, breathe

Losing a job hurts at any time. With a new baby it can feel like the ground just gave way. Shame, panic and anger are all normal. A lot of dads feel they’ve failed their family. You haven’t. Layoffs happen to good workers every day.

The first week: what to do

  • File for unemployment right away. Don’t wait. Each state runs its own. CareerOneStop links to yours.
  • Sort out health insurance. Losing a job opens a 60-day window to sign up at HealthCare.gov. You may also be offered COBRA, which keeps your old plan but usually costs a lot more. Compare both.
  • Check Medicaid and CHIP for the baby. Many kids qualify for free or low-cost coverage when income drops. HealthCare.gov will tell you when you apply.
  • Ask about severance and your last paycheck, and any unused vacation pay.
  • Write down every bill and its due date. Call before you miss one. Many companies have hardship plans if you ask early.

Help with food and diapers

  • WIC helps with formula, food and breastfeeding support for babies, kids under 5 and new moms. Many families who just lost income qualify. Dads, stepdads and foster dads can apply for their baby or child under 5 too. You don’t have to be the mom.
  • SNAP (food stamps) is through your state. Call 211 and they’ll point you to it, plus diaper banks and food banks near you.
  • See money stress with a new baby for more.

Tell your partner soon

Keeping it quiet to protect her usually backfires. Say it plainly, then share the plan, even if it’s only the first step. Copy this if the words won’t come.

“I got some hard news today. I was let go. I'm scared, but I've already started on unemployment and insurance. Can we sit down tonight and make a plan together?”

While you’re home

Being home with a baby and no job can mess with your head. Give the days some shape.

  • Set work hours for the job search, like a job. Then stop.
  • Take on a real share of the baby. This time is hard, but it’s also time with your kid that most dads never get.
  • Get outside every day. A walk with the stroller counts.
  • Tell people you’re looking. Most jobs come through someone you know.

Watch your mood

Job loss is one of the biggest triggers for depression in men. If you’re sleeping all day, can’t sleep at all, drinking more or feel like nothing matters for more than two weeks, talk to a doctor. No insurance right now? See getting help without insurance. A lost job is hard to bring up with friends, and other dads who have been there can help. PSI’s free dads’ groups meet online, and our message board is open any time.

If it gets dark

If you’re thinking about suicide, or that your family would be better off without you, call or text 988. It’s free, 24/7, and you don’t have to be in crisis to call. If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

Common questions

What should I do first if I lose my job with a new baby?

File for unemployment right away, sort out health insurance within 60 days through HealthCare.gov or COBRA, check Medicaid or CHIP for the baby, and write down every bill and due date.

Can my baby get health insurance if I lose my job?

Many kids qualify for free or low-cost coverage through Medicaid or CHIP when income drops. Apply through HealthCare.gov and it will tell you.

Where can we get help with formula and food?

WIC helps with formula and food for babies, kids under 5 and new moms. Call 211 for SNAP, diaper banks and food banks near you.
