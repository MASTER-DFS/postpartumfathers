Getting help

Getting help when you don't have insurance

No insurance, or insurance you can't afford to use, is one of the most common reasons dads put off getting help. There's more free and low-cost help out there than most people know. Here's where to start.

If you're ever thinking about suicide, please call or text 988 any time, day or night. It's free and you don't need insurance. If anyone is in danger right now, call 911. More crisis help.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Start here

  • If tonight is hard, call or text 988 or 1-833-852-6262. Both are free, 24/7, and need no insurance.
  • Tomorrow, call 211 and say "I need low-cost counseling." They'll find what's near you.
  • Look up a community health center. They charge by income and see people without insurance.

Free ways to talk to someone

None of these need insurance, and none of them cost anything.

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Call or text 988, any time. You don't have to be suicidal to call. Feeling overwhelmed is reason enough.
  • National Maternal Mental Health Hotline. Call or text 1-833-852-6262, 24/7. Trained counselors, and dads can call too.
  • PSI HelpLine. Call 1-800-944-4773 or text "Help" to the same number. A volunteer calls you back and gives you names of support near you. It's not a crisis line; they reply every day, 8am to 11pm Eastern.
  • NAMI HelpLine. Call 1-800-950-6264 or text "NAMI" to 62640, Monday to Friday, 10am to 10pm Eastern. Information and referrals, not a crisis line.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline. Call 1-800-662-4357, 24/7, for referrals to mental health care and help with drinking or drugs, including places for people without insurance.
  • Free dads' groups. PSI's online groups for dads are free, and so is our message board. See free support groups for dads.

Low-cost care near you

  • Call 211. It's confidential and connects you to local help with healthcare, counseling, rent, food and more. Say: "I need low-cost counseling." Or search 211.org.
  • Community health centers. These are federally funded clinics that charge on a sliding scale based on your income, and they see people even without insurance. Many offer mental health care, so ask. Find a health center near you.
  • Your county's mental health department. Most counties run free or low-cost services. 211 can give you the number.
  • Therapists with a sliding scale. Many lower their fee if you ask. Search the PSI directory or FindTreatment.gov, then ask when you call.
  • University training clinics. Counseling and psychology programs often see the public at a low fee, with supervised trainees.
  • Your job. Many employers offer an Employee Assistance Program with a few free, confidential sessions, sometimes even if you're not on the company health plan. Ask HR.
  • Open Path Collective (paid). A nonprofit that connects you with therapists at reduced rates, online or in person, after a one-time membership fee. Prices are on their site.

Find help in your state

These let you search by your state or ZIP code:

  • 211.org: enter your ZIP code for local help with counseling, healthcare, food, rent and more.
  • Find a Health Center: community health centers near your address, with fees based on income.
  • PSI Find Local Support: Postpartum Support International has local support coordinators in every US state who know what's near you.
  • FindTreatment.gov: mental health and substance use care near you, from the federal government.
  • Medicaid and CHIP by state: pick your state to find who to call about coverage.

Help in Spanish

If you, or a dad you're helping, would rather talk in Spanish, these are free:

  • 988: call 988 and press 2, or text "AYUDA" to 988.
  • Maternal Mental Health Hotline: call 1-833-852-6262 and press 2.
  • PSI HelpLine: call 1-800-944-4773 and press 1, or text 971-203-7773. PSI also runs free Spanish support groups.
  • Crisis Text Line: text "AYUDA" to 741741.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357, in English and Spanish.
  • 211 has a Spanish site.

Getting covered

  • Medicaid. You can apply any time of year, not just in open enrollment. If money is tight, it's worth checking. Start at HealthCare.gov or your state's Medicaid office.
  • A new baby opens a window. If your family doesn't have a plan, the birth lets you sign up for a Marketplace plan at HealthCare.gov. You have 60 days from the birth.
  • Your baby may qualify on their own. Medicaid and CHIP cover many children even when their parents aren't covered.

Medicine on a budget

Many common antidepressants have been around for years and come as low-cost generics. If a doctor suggests one, ask for the generic and ask the pharmacy for the cash price. Community health centers can also help with lower-cost prescriptions. More on what treatment actually looks like.

What to say when you call

  • "I don't have insurance. Do you have a sliding scale, or can you point me to somewhere that does?"
  • "I'm a new dad and I haven't felt like myself since the baby. I'm looking for low-cost counseling."
  • "What's the cheapest way to be seen? Is there a payment plan?"

Asking about cost is normal. Clinics hear it all day, and nobody will think less of you for it.

Not having insurance doesn't mean you have to wait until things get worse. Start with one call. Next, see finding a therapist, and paying for it, how to talk to your doctor, or money stress and a new baby.

Common questions

Can I get mental health help without insurance?

Yes. 988, the Maternal Mental Health Hotline and the PSI HelpLine are free. Community health centers charge by income and see people without insurance, and many therapists offer a sliding scale.

Where can I find free or low-cost therapy near me?

Call 211 and ask for low-cost counseling, look up a community health center, call your county's mental health department, or ask therapists in the PSI directory about a sliding scale.

Is there help in Spanish?

Yes. Call 988 and press 2, call 1-833-852-6262 and press 2, or call the PSI HelpLine at 1-800-944-4773 and press 1. You can also text AYUDA to 741741.

Can I get insurance after having a baby?

If your family has no plan, the birth opens a 60-day window to sign up at HealthCare.gov. You can apply for Medicaid any time of year.
