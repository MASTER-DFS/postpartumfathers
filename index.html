Postpartum depression support for dads

You're not the only dad feeling this way.

Postpartum depression doesn't only happen to moms, and it can knock fathers flat too. Whether you're struggling yourself or watching your wife or partner struggle, you deserve support, and you don't have to figure it out alone.

Start with what you're facing

"Something's off with me."

Anger, numbness, working late to avoid going home. Depression in dads often doesn't look like sadness. It's common and it's treatable.

Visit the community
1 in 10

1 in 10 new fathers experience depression during pregnancy or in the first year after birth.

Dads whose partners have postpartum depression face an even higher risk. Many never hear that it can happen to them, so they don't recognize it or ask for help.

Source: Paulson & Bazemore, JAMA, 2010, a review of 43 studies.

Signs to watch for

It doesn't always look like depression

If several of these have lasted more than two weeks, it's worth talking to a doctor or therapist.

  • Short temper, irritability, or anger
  • Pulling away from your partner or baby
  • Working more to avoid home
  • More drinking, gaming, or scrolling
  • Feeling numb, trapped, or hopeless
  • Can't sleep even when the baby does
  • Constant worry or racing thoughts
  • Feeling like your family would be better off without you

If you've thought your family would be better off without you, that one is serious. Please call or text 988 now.

Struggling after a baby doesn't make you a bad father or a weak one. It means you need some backup, and that's what this place is for.
