New dad anxiety

Anxious as a new dad? You’re not alone, and it gets easier

Checking the baby is still breathing. Running through every "what if" at 2am. Feeling on edge all day. If that sounds like you, you're in good company. Anxiety is one of the most common things new fathers go through, and it's very treatable.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

Try this tonight

  • Slow your breathing. In for 4, hold for 4, out for 6. A few rounds.
  • Name the worry. Say it out loud or write it down.
  • Put the phone down. Searching symptoms at 2am usually feeds the worry.

Postpartum Support International says 5 to 15 percent of dads develop an anxiety disorder, such as generalized anxiety, during pregnancy or the baby's first year. It's common, and it's treatable.

What it can feel like

  • Worry you can't switch off
  • Checking on the baby again and again
  • A racing heart, tight chest, or shaky hands
  • Trouble falling asleep even when you get the chance
  • Feeling tense, snappy or on edge
  • Dreading money, work, or something going wrong
  • Sudden, unwanted thoughts of the baby getting hurt
  • Avoiding things because of the worry

Those sudden, unwanted thoughts are more common than most dads realize. They tend to horrify the people who have them, which is a sign of how much you care. They don't mean you will act on them, and they ease with help. Telling your doctor about them is safe and helps.

Why it happens

A new baby turns your whole life upside down overnight. You're more responsible than you've ever been, on less sleep than you've ever had, often while still working full time. A worried brain is trying to protect your family. It just needs help turning the volume down.

Simple things that help

  • Slow your breathing. Breathe in for 4, hold for 4, out for 6. A few rounds can calm a racing body.
  • Name the worry. Say it out loud or write it down. Worries shrink when they're out of your head.
  • Move a little. A short walk, even with the stroller, burns off some of the tension.
  • Go easy on caffeine and alcohol. Both can make anxiety and sleep worse.
  • Put the phone down at night. Searching symptoms at 2am usually feeds the worry.
  • Talk to another dad. Our message board is open any time, under a username.

When to talk to someone

If the worry has lasted more than two weeks, gets in the way of sleep, work or enjoying your family, or comes with panic attacks, talk to your doctor. Anxiety in new parents responds well to treatment.

Here's how to bring it up with your doctor. You can also call the PSI HelpLine at 1-800-944-4773 for support and referrals, or find a therapist who works with new parents in the PSI directory. Therapy usually costs money; ask about insurance or a sliding scale.

Common questions

Can new dads get anxiety after a baby?

Yes. Anxiety is common in new fathers, during pregnancy and in the first year. It is real and very treatable.

Is it normal to have scary thoughts about the baby getting hurt?

Sudden, unwanted thoughts like this are common in new parents, including dads. They don't mean you will act on them. If they worry you, tell your doctor or call the PSI HelpLine at 1-800-944-4773. If you ever feel you might act on a thought, call or text 988 now.

When should a dad get help for anxiety?

If the worry lasts more than two weeks, disrupts sleep, work or family life, or comes with panic attacks, talk to a doctor.

What if I need help tonight?

Call or text 988 any time. The Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262 also helps dads and partners, 24/7. In an emergency, call 911.
