Money worries

Money stress with a new baby? Here’s where to start

Diapers, formula, childcare, maybe a smaller paycheck while someone's on leave. Money worries can keep a new dad up at night as much as the baby does. It's one of the most common stresses for fathers, and there's more help out there than most people know about.

Rough night? Call or text 988, or 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, dads too).

First steps

  • Look at it on paper. Write down what's coming in and going out. Worry is usually bigger in your head than on the page.
  • Find the big three. Housing, food and childcare first. Everything else can flex for a while.
  • Call before you fall behind. Many landlords, lenders and utility companies have hardship plans if you ask early.
  • Check your benefits at work. Ask HR about parental leave pay, dependent care accounts, and any family benefits you haven't used.
  • Accept hand-me-downs. Babies outgrow things fast, and lots of parents are happy to pass them on.

Free places to find help

  • Dial 2-1-1. Call 211 to be connected with local help for food, rent, utilities and more. It's free and confidential.
  • WIC. The government's WIC program helps many families with babies and young children get food, formula and support. Ask your baby's doctor or call 211 to see if you qualify.
  • Your baby's doctor or hospital often knows about local programs for new families.

This week, try one or two

  • Write down every bill due in the next 30 days and the date it’s due.
  • Make one call about a payment plan, before anything is late.
  • Cancel one thing you don’t use, like a subscription or app.
  • Check if you qualify for WIC. Call 211 or ask your baby’s doctor.
  • Ask about hospital bills. Many hospitals have financial assistance programs. Call the billing office and ask before you pay.

Stay away from payday loans and high-interest quick cash. They tend to make the hole deeper. Small steps add up, and you don’t have to fix it all this week.

When the stress takes over

If money worry keeps you from sleeping, makes you snap at people, or leaves you feeling hopeless, that's a lot to carry, and it's worth getting support for you too. Read worn down as a new dad and anxiety in new dads. If you ever feel your family would be better off without you, please call or text 988 now.

Common questions

How do new dads cope with money stress?

Write down income and costs, focus on housing, food and childcare first, call creditors early about hardship plans, and check workplace benefits.

Where can families with a new baby get financial help?

Dial 211 to be connected to local help for food, rent and utilities. The WIC program helps many families with infants get food and formula.

What if money stress is affecting my mental health?

Talk to someone. Constant worry, irritability or hopelessness are worth getting support for. If you feel your family would be better off without you, call or text 988.
